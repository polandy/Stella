## 2.13 Important dates & reminders **[M2]**

A date is either on the household's radar or it is not — there is no separate reminder
object to create, schedule and maintain. And there is no reminders *screen*: what is coming
up appears where the household already looks, at the top of the Home stream (§2.22.2), so
the stream reads as one timeline with the future above the past.

### 2.13.1 The dates themselves

- A contact can have **important dates**: `birthday`, `anniversary` or `custom` (a custom
  one must be named, or it means nothing three months later). Each carries whether it
  **recurs yearly** and whether it should **remind** — the "show it on Home" switch.
- A date may be a full ISO day or a year-less `--MM-DD` when the year is unknown; the age
  or count is then simply omitted rather than guessed. **Leaving the year blank** in the date
  field is how that is said — there is no separate "year unknown" switch, because the empty
  segment already means it (§5.7).
- Dates are **child records of a contact** with no visibility of their own: they inherit
  the contact's, enforced by the adapter's scoped reads (§2.10).
- Dates are listed, added and removed in the **dates editor** that every date fact of the
  person page's identity card opens in place (§2.2) — each date is a fact of its own there.

### 2.13.2 Birthdays are derived, never duplicated

A birthday comes from `contact.birth_date`, captured on the contact form. No
`important_date` row is written for it, so the two can never disagree.

An **explicit `birthday` row overrides the derived one** for that contact. That is the one
mechanism behind two features: correcting a birthday without touching the profile, and
**muting** one — an explicit birthday row with `remind` off silences it.

### 2.13.3 What Home shows

- The **Coming up** band in Home's rail (§2.12) lists the next occurrences inside a 30-day
  horizon, soonest first, capped at five. It is **absent entirely when nothing is due** — a permanently
  empty panel teaches people to stop looking.
- Whether a date is **within 14 days** also decides where the rail stands on a phone: above
  the stream while something is that close, below it otherwise (docs/05 §5.5).
- Each entry reads as a countdown ("today", "tomorrow", "in 4 days") rather than a
  calendar entry, and says what the occasion is ("turns 11", "12 years together").
- Each entry offers exactly **one action: write a moment about it** — `/?about=<contactId>`
  opens the capture field with that person's handle already in it (§2.22.1). This is the
  point of a reminder: it closes back into the app's core verb instead of just informing.
- A person with **open gift ideas** the reader may see adds a quiet line, *2 ideas* with the
  gift icon, linking to their Gifts card (§2.25.5) — a hint where the occasion already is,
  not a second action.
- **Deceased contacts are excluded** (§2.2), and a one-off date disappears once it passes.
- 29 February falls on **1 March** in a common year, so an anniversary is never skipped.

### 2.13.4 In the phone's calendar

A member subscribes their phone's own calendar to a secret link, and the calendar does the lead
times and reminding — Stella sends no notifications of its own.

- **Settings → Calendar** (§2.17): *Create calendar link* shows the link
  `<ORIGIN>/cal/<secret>.ics` **once**, with *Copy* and how to add it in Google Calendar
  (*From URL*), on an iPhone (*Add Subscribed Calendar*) and elsewhere (Thunderbird,
  DAVx⁵/ICSx⁵). It is kept hashed, so it cannot be shown again; a member who lost it creates a
  new one. Afterwards the page shows when the link was made and last fetched.
- **One link per member.** *Create a new link…* replaces it and *Revoke…* withdraws it, each
  after a confirm step; both take effect at once, with no undo — a link is replaced because it
  may be in the wrong hands. Removing a member ends their link with their tokens (§2.1).
- **What it holds:** what *Coming up* would show the member, without the 30-day horizon —
  every date with *Show on Home* on, of every person the member may see. Deceased and archived
  people stay out, a private person is only in their owner's feed, an explicit birthday
  replaces the derived one. To-dos are not in it.
- **Each date is one all-day event**, marked free so it never blocks a day. A recurring date
  repeats yearly from its first year (a year-less one from 2000); 29 February repeats on the
  **last day of February**, so a common year shows it on the 28th (Home's band says 1 March —
  a calendar rule cannot say "the day after February"). A one-off date is a single event, a
  year-less one-off none. The title is in the member's language — *Anna's birthday*, *Anna's
  anniversary*, *Anna: Name day* — and the description carries *Born 2015* / *Since 2010* and a
  link to the person's page. An event's id is stable, so an edited date moves rather than
  doubles. No alarms are set: reminders are each member's setting in their calendar.
- The answer is `text/calendar`, `Cache-Control: private, max-age=3600`, tagged with its
  content so an unchanged feed costs a 304. An unknown or revoked link is a plain 404.

### 2.13.5 Later

- Optional **email reminders** to members who opt in **[M3]**. No push spam.
- Custom lead times ("two weeks before") and snoozing are deliberately out of scope until
  the simple version has been lived with — the subscribed calendar (§2.13.4) has both.
