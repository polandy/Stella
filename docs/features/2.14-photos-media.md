## 2.14 Photos & media **[M1 avatar / M2 gallery]**

- Each contact has an **avatar** and a **photo gallery**.
- Upload from desktop or mobile camera/roll. **Avatars are processed in the browser** (M1):
  the client applies EXIF orientation, cuts the square the person chose, and produces a full +
  a thumbnail JPEG; re-encoding via canvas **drops all EXIF/GPS metadata** (privacy) and keeps
  uploads small, so the server needs no native image library. The server validates (magic
  bytes, size) and stores both variants on the media volume; dimensions/size in the DB.
- EXIF orientation respected; EXIF GPS/personal metadata **stripped** by the client re-encode,
  for avatars and gallery photos alike. The capture date is the one tag kept — read before the
  re-encode (below).
- Photos have their own visibility (2.10) and can be captioned.
- Basic gallery: grid, lightbox, set-as-avatar, delete. Favourites first **[M3]** instead
  of a free reordering, which is not planned.

- **Shipped:** the person page carries a **Photos** tab — a square grid, newest first by capture date (favourites ahead, below), each
  private photo wearing a lock. Adding takes several files at once; each is downscaled and
  re-encoded in the browser first, so no EXIF or GPS ever leaves the device, and the upload
  carries its own *shared or private* choice. Opening one gives a **lightbox** over a dimmed
  backdrop: the picture, its caption, *Use as photo* (which makes it the contact's avatar),
  the shared/private switch and *Remove*. Escape closes it and the arrow keys — or the previous
  and next buttons — walk the grid — except while the caption field has focus, where they move the caret — and closing hands focus back to the photo's thumbnail.
  Captioning and re-scoping appear only on photos you added yourself (§2.10); anyone who can
  see a photo can make it the avatar. *Remove* is the author's, and a household admin's on a
  shared photo — the rule notes follow (§2.5, docs/03 §3.7), never a private photo of someone
  else's. It goes through the held undo window (§2.23): the photo leaves the grid at once and
  *Undo* is on offer for eight seconds. A photo the person wears says so in the toast
  (*Photo removed — also Kurt's picture*). When the window closes, both stored variants are
  deleted, the framing goes with them and, if the contact was wearing it, the avatar is cleared
  in the same transaction. Removing needs a connection, and the right is checked again then. Journal photos (§2.20) stay in Activity and never appear here.
- **Shipped: choosing the square.** Picking a new photo for someone opens a **cropper** before
  anything is uploaded: the picture sits behind a round window the shape of the avatar, starting
  on the centred square the uploader used to cut on its own. Drag moves it, pinch or the mouse
  wheel zooms around the fingers or the pointer, and a slider and the arrow keys and + / − do
  the same without a gesture. The picture can never be moved or zoomed out far enough to leave
  empty space in the square, and zoom stops where the square is about 256 px of the picture
  itself — far into a 4096 px class photo, barely into a small one — rather than at a fixed
  factor, so a face can fill the frame without being blown up past its detail. *Use photo*
  uploads exactly the square shown;
  Escape or *Cancel* uploads nothing. The geometry is pure and tested (`src/lib/media/crop.ts`),
  and the canvas cuts from the same numbers the dialog draws with.
- **Shipped: framing a gallery photo.** *Use as photo* in the lightbox opens the same cropper
  on the full picture. The gallery keeps **one** photo: the chosen square is remembered on a
  *framing* of it (docs/03 §photo), and that is what the person wears. On the photo worn now
  the button reads *Change framing*, and on any photo framed before the cropper starts on the
  square chosen last time, not the centre. Choosing again replaces the framing rather than
  adding one; removing the photo removes its framing, and a framing follows its photo to
  private. The archive carries a framing with the square and the photo it belongs to, and a
  restore that has to refuse a photo refuses its framing too.
- **Shipped: profile pictures cut from a group photo** (ADR-116). A
  circle photo can be framed too, once **per person**: a *cut* is a framing of the circle photo
  that belongs to the person who wears it, rendered at 1024 px rather than 512 so it can later
  stand on its own. Cutting the same photo again for the same person replaces their cut. It
  starts from the circle photo's lightbox (*Use as profile picture for …*, §2.4.2) or from the
  person's own round picture: when one of their circles holds a photo the viewer can see,
  choosing a picture offers those group photos beside a file, and a pick opens the cropper on
  the full group photo. A cut **never just disappears**. When the person gets another picture —
  an upload, a framing of a gallery photo, a cut of another group photo — the cut they wore
  becomes a **photo of their own**, dated like the group photo and marked *From Class 1B* with a
  link to the circle; the row is turned into a gallery photo, nothing is re-encoded. So
  everything someone ever wore as a profile picture ends up in their gallery. When the
  group photo is **removed** or made **private**, the uploader is told how many people wear it,
  and every cut becomes its person's own photo — shared, in the private case — still worn, in the
  same transaction that removes or re-scopes the group photo. The Photos tab then shows *On
  group photos*: every group photo the person was ever cut from, now or before, with its
  circle's name and a tap into the circle; one that is gone or that the viewer cannot see is
  left out. A cut is a change, not an addition, so it is never kept on the device to send later
  (§2.18). The rules — which cuts a removal turns, which cut a new picture leaves behind, how
  such a photo is dated — are pure in `src/lib/server/domain/media/cuts.ts`.
- **Shipped: photo history.** Every gallery photo shows its day, on the tile and next to its
  caption in the lightbox — the day it was taken when known (below), else the day it was added;
  legible at a glance for a face that visibly changes year to year, a child especially. Choosing a new avatar for someone who already had one never
  discards the old one: setting an avatar has always stored it as a fresh gallery photo rather
  than overwriting anything, so the previous picture simply drops back into the dated gallery,
  and a toast confirms it at the moment of the change.
- **Shipped: photos of a circle.** A circle has photos of its own, each optionally carrying one
  of its roles (§2.4.2): they belong to the circle and to nobody's gallery. They are processed
  in the browser like a person's gallery photos (EXIF-free), but kept up to **4096 px** on the
  longest edge so faces can be cut from them, with a **1600 px view** stored beside the full
  picture and the thumbnail: the grid loads the thumbnail, the strips and the lightbox the view, only the
  cropper loads the full picture (`src/lib/media/renditions.ts`). Shared or private,
  captioned and pinned, and ordered by the same rule (`gallery-order.ts`). Unlike here,
  their caption, role and pin are for anyone who can see them — on purpose, a class photo's
  caption is the class's — while re-scoping stays with the uploader. *Remove* is the
  uploader's, and an admin's on a shared photo, held for the undo window like a person's photo;
  the question about people who wear it comes first.
- **Shipped: the capture date.** Before the browser re-encodes a picture it reads one thing out
  of the file: when it was taken. A small pure reader (`src/lib/media/exif-date.ts`, no
  dependency) walks a JPEG's APP1 EXIF to **DateTimeOriginal** with its **OffsetTimeOriginal**,
  and falls back to **DateTimeDigitized** (with its offset) when the original is missing or
  blank — the same moment on a camera, the scan's on a scanned print. IFD0's DateTime is not
  used: it is when the file was last changed. GPS and every other tag are never read and go with
  the re-encode as before. HEIC, PNG and a JPEG without EXIF have no date, and nothing changes
  for them. The date is kept as the camera wrote it — `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS`, plus the offset when
  there is one (docs/03 §photo) — so the day shown is the photographer's calendar day wherever it
  is looked at; ordering reads one without an offset as UTC, hours at most from the truth. A date
  that cannot be real — before 1826 or more than a day past now, a camera whose clock was never
  set — is dropped on the device, so an upload never fails over it.
  It travels beside the picture as `takenAt` on every upload path: a person's gallery, a new
  avatar (which lands in the gallery), journal photos, circle photos, and the Monica import (its
  files are the originals, so their EXIF is still there); a photo kept on the phone while offline
  keeps it (§2.18.1). The server refuses a `takenAt` that does not read
  (the command is malformed) or cannot be real against its clock, and stores one that can in
  `photo.taken_at`; most photos carry none, which is normal. A photo is then **dated** by its
  capture when known, else by when it was added — one rule (`datedAt`, `src/lib/media/taken-at.ts`)
  that orders the person's gallery, a circle's photos and its cover, and the person's *On group
  photos*. The lightbox says *Taken 3 May 2019* when the date came from the picture; a circle
  photo's says that beside *Added … by …*. The household stream keeps the day of the upload: it
  tells what happened when. Journal photos keep the order they were attached in, under their
  entry's own day. The archive carries `taken_at`.
- **Shipped: favourite photos first.** In the lightbox, *Pin as favourite* makes a photo one of
  the person's favourites, and *Unpin favourite* takes it back. Favourites lead the gallery,
  the one pinned most recently first, so a pin visibly puts that photo at the front, and
  unpinning and pinning again moves a favourite there; everything else follows, newest first —
  by capture date where known.
  A favourite wears a star on its tile and *Favourite*, with the star, beside its date in the
  lightbox — a shape and a word, never only a colour. A pin is the **household's**, like the
  photo (docs/04 §4.9): anyone who can see a photo can pin or unpin it, and everyone who sees it
  sees it pinned. Pinning a photo that is already pinned keeps its first pin, so a resent form
  changes nothing. It is a change, not an addition, so it is not queued offline
  (§2.18.1). The rule is one pure function
  (`src/lib/server/domain/media/gallery-order.ts`) and the gallery is the only list of a
  person's photos it orders: journal photos stay with their moment in Activity. The archive
  carries the pin (`pinned_at`), so a restore brings the favourites back.

- **Shipped: one card for both sources.** The Photos card's segmented control reads *All ·
  Stella 5 · Immich 1,764* (Immich only for a linked person, once Immich has answered). *All*
  is a glance — one row of seven tiles on a wide card, two rows of three on a phone, the last
  tile *All 1,769 photos* once there are more — mixing the gallery with the person's latest
  Immich photos: favourites first, then newest first by when each was taken, an Immich tile
  badged *Immich*. That tile opens *All* out in place; *Stella* is the whole gallery and
  *Immich* the Immich photos, twelve more per *Show more*. A gallery photo older than every
  Immich photo loaded so far waits until *Show more* reaches its date, so the list only grows at
  its end. One lightbox serves both: it walks the list its tile was opened from, says where the
  photo lives and *3 of 12*, and leads with *Use as photo*. The mix and the cap are pure in
  `src/lib/people/photo-card.ts`.
- **Photos kept in Immich** stay there: a person linked to Immich shows how many photos Immich
  has of them under the grid, and the Photos card's menu links or unlinks them (§2.24). *Use
  as photo* on one of their Immich photos opens the same cropper on Immich's preview, and the
  square becomes their photo like any new avatar, dated as Immich dates it (§2.24.6). With Immich
  on the instance, tapping the person's picture always opens the chooser — a file, their group
  photos, and *From Immich* (§2.24.6); without Immich and without group photos it still opens the
  file picker at once. The rule is pure in `src/lib/people/avatar-chooser.ts`.
