## 2.20 Personal journal (per-person diary) **[M2]**

A **journal** is a per-person diary: household members chronicle a contact's life day by day —
a child's milestones, a friend's big moments — in a warm, readable timeline. It is distinct
from **notes** (§2.5): notes are reference facts about a person ("allergic to peanuts",
"met at Anna's wedding"); journal entries are dated diary moments ("today she took her first
steps in the garden").

- **Called a moment.** The UI calls an entry a **moment** (*Write a moment*, *Save moment*);
  *Journal* is the page they are kept on (§2.22.1).
- **Written on the person's page, read on the journal page.** The person page's *Write a
  moment* opens the moment composer (§2.22.1) in place, at the top of the *Activity* card,
  anchored on that person: the moment lands in their journal without an `@`, the anchor shows
  as a chip above the field (*· goes to Markus's journal*), and `@` offers everyone else —
  naming the person themselves would be the entry's subject, not a mention (§2.20.1). Saved, it
  appears at the top of the card's timeline and joins that day's entry like any other addition
  (below). The journal page (`/contacts/{id}/journal`, the card's *Open journal*) stays the
  long-form reading and editing surface, with a *Write a moment* of its own.
- **Per day.** Each entry is *about* a specific day (`entry_date`, an ISO date), separate from
  when it was written. The profile shows entries as a timeline, newest day first, grouped by
  date and rendered from Markdown (safe render, same pipeline as notes — raw HTML escaped,
  unsafe links dropped).
- **Markdown, rendered beautifully.** Authors write in Markdown; it is server-rendered to safe
  HTML for a nice reading view.
- **Photos.** An entry can carry photos (§2.14 media pipeline: browser-side downscale + EXIF/GPS
  strip on upload, stored on the media volume, served through the authenticated `/media` route).
  Images render inline in the entry. With Immich connected, the photo button offers *From this
  device* and *From Immich*, the library opening on the entry's day (§2.24.9).
- **Visibility (§2.10).** Each entry is **shared** (the whole household sees this person's
  journal) or **private** (only the author). Default is the author's household default. A
  private entry is a genuine diary — no one else, admin included, can read it.
- **One entry per slot.** Uniqueness is per **(people, author, day, visibility)** — the people
  being the entry's subjects (one, or several, §2.20.3): writing on
  the same day again *adds* to that entry — a new paragraph, its mentions joining the entry's,
  the title kept (an untitled day takes the new one) — rather than duplicating or replacing it,
  so "one entry per day" holds while still letting a member keep both a shared and a separate
  private entry for the same day. An entry kept on a phone and sent days later therefore never
  overwrites what was written meanwhile.
- **Ownership.** Entries are attributed to their author; you may edit **your own**, and delete it — a household admin may delete a **shared** entry of someone else's too (docs/03 §3.7), never edit it. A removal by someone other than the author is logged for the household (§2.11).
  Editing changes the title/body in place — the day and visibility stay put, since they are
  part of the entry's identity (its day-slot, above); to move an entry to another day or change
  its audience, delete it and write a new one. An edit down to nothing but spaces is refused
  with a request for text, not saved; an entry someone deleted meanwhile — in another tab — is
  answered as gone by an edit and a delete alike, never as done.
- Implemented as a pure, test-first domain module (`domain/journal`) over a `JournalRepository`
  port; visibility-scoped reads live in the Drizzle adapter; the route is a thin edge.
- Monica's **journal** entries map here on import (§2.16); other Monica free-text falls back to
  notes.

### 2.20.1 @-mentions of people (passive references) **[M2]**

Inside a journal entry you can reference another person already in Stella. The reference is
**clickable** when rendered, and it surfaces on the referenced person as a **passive entry** —
they didn't get an entry of their own, they were *mentioned* in someone else's — showing which
person's journal it came from. Example: in *Beat*'s journal you write "hiked with `@SandraBrunner`";
on *Sandra*'s profile a passive item appears: "mentioned in *Beat Steiner*'s journal, 12 Jul".

- **Authoring.** Typing `@` opens an autocomplete over the contacts you may see (§2.10),
  filtered by name as you type; picking one inserts the mention. The on-screen form is
  `@FirstnameLastname` with no space, e.g. `@AnnaWeber`. Write `\@` for a literal "@". The
  field remembers whom each picked handle stands for, through any typing around it, and saves
  it as that person's token; changing the name itself lets the pick go (§2.2.3). Editing an
  entry shows its tokens as handles again, still remembered, so saving an edit keeps every
  mention it had.
- **Stored form is id-based, not name-based.** A confirmed mention is saved as a stable token
  that embeds the contact **id** — so it survives a later rename and never resolves to the wrong
  person when two people share a name. The typed `@AnnaWeber` is only the lookup key. A raw,
  unconfirmed `@FirstnameLastname` (e.g. pasted text) is resolved when the entry is saved: a
  single exact first+last match becomes a mention, an unmatched one is left as literal text
  (`@home` is often not a mention at all), and one that could be several people is refused with
  their names rather than guessed or dropped (§2.2.3). The id inside the token may be a *source id* from an import
  (`monica:contact:9`, §2.16), so the token grammar accepts `:` in it.
- **Rendering.** A mention renders as a chip/link to `/contacts/{id}`, labelled with the
  person's **current** display name (looked up at render time). It goes through the same
  safe-render pipeline as the rest of the body (raw HTML escaped, unsafe links dropped); the
  mention chip is the only non-Markdown token the renderer injects.
- **Passive appearance on the referenced person.** On the mentioned contact's profile a
  **"Mentioned in"** list shows every entry that references them, newest first. Each item names
  the **source person** (whose journal — "in *Beat Steiner*'s journal"), the **author**, the
  **entry date**, and a title/snippet, and links to that entry. It is **read-only** here: the
  entry is owned and edited on the source person's journal. A self-mention (an entry in a
  person's own journal that references that same person) creates no passive item.
- **Visibility (§2.10) — a mention never widens access.** A passive item is shown only to
  viewers who may already see the **source entry** (a private entry ⇒ only its author), the
  **source contact**, and the **referenced contact**. To stop a mention leaking a private
  person's existence, a **shared** entry may reference only contacts the whole household can
  see; a **private** entry may reference anyone its author can see. The autocomplete only offers
  contacts allowed for the entry's current audience.
- **Lifecycle.** Saving or editing an entry re-parses its body and rebuilds that entry's mention
  links to match (`journal_mention`, §3.3); deleting the entry removes them (cascade). Because
  only the id is stored, renaming the referenced person updates every chip automatically.
- **One shared pipeline.** Notes carry the same soft-link idea (§2.5, `note_mention`); the
  parser, id-resolver, and chip renderer are shared between notes and journal rather than
  duplicated. Framework-agnostic domain module, test-first (`docs/08` §8.3). The `@`-picker is
  one component too (`MentionTextarea`, `docs/05` §5.7) — the moment composer keeps its own
  because only a moment may create a person on the fly. The two rules that decide *whom* a text
  may name live there as well, so the browser and the server never drift: the audience rule
  above (`allowedForAudience`) and the subject rule (`mentionsOtherThan`), which keeps the
  person a note or entry is about from being a passive reference to themselves.
- **Search follows the name, not the token.** Because the body stores an id, the search index
  carries the mentioned people's current display names in the token's place, so a note that
  says only "walked home with @Sandra" is still found by *Sandra* — and renaming her moves the
  index with her (`docs/03` §3.5).
- **Where it is read.** *Mentioned in* is the last card on a person's page, under Photos,
  carrying the exact number of references; with none it is not on the page (docs/05 §5.5). Notes and journal entries are listed together, newest
  first — a reference reads the same whichever it came from — and each item names the source
  person, who wrote it, the day, and a one-line preview in which the mention tokens read as the
  people's current names. Following an item opens the source person at the card the entry lives
  on (`#section-notes` or `#section-story`); it is not edited here. A private entry is marked as such, and only its author ever sees it
  in the list at all.

### 2.20.2 Tags on moments **[M3]**

A moment can carry **tags** — topics to find it by a year from now (*#hike*, *#school*,
*#Geburtstag*). They are the household's **own set**, apart from the people tags (§2.8): a
person is *family*, a moment is about *a hike*.

- **Written in the text.** A tag is `#word` anywhere in a moment's text: one word of letters
  (umlauts included), digits, `-` and `_`, with at least one letter — `#2024` is a number, not
  a tag. A `#` inside a word (`mail#x`) or in code is not a tag, and `\#` writes a literal
  `#`. Notes carry none.
- **Offered while typing.** Typing `#` in the moment composer (Home, the person page) or the
  journal page's fields opens a list of the household's tags matching what follows, each as its
  chip with how many moments carry it, and last a *New tag* row for the word as typed; arrows,
  Enter or Tab, and Escape work as in the `@` list. Under the field a line names the tags the
  moment will be saved with, a tag nobody has yet marked *new*; with none, it hints *Add a #tag
  to find this moment by topic later*.
- **One tag, whatever the case.** `#Hike` and `#hike` are one tag: names are matched with case
  and composition folded, and the spelling it was **first** written in is the one shown. Its
  colour is derived from the name, one of the accent set — nothing to pick.
- **Rebuilt from the text.** Saving, editing, adding to a day's entry, a restore and a merge
  all re-read the entry's tags from its text; a tag no moment carries any more is gone with
  the write that dropped it. Moments written before tags existed are read once, on the first
  start of a Stella that has them.
- **Read as chips.** In a moment's text a tag renders as a small chip in its colour. On a
  person's journal page it filters that journal; on Home's stream, a person's *Activity* and a
  tag's page it opens the tag's page.
- **Filtered on the journal page.** Above the journal's timeline a row of chips — *All* and
  each tag on this person's moments, with its count — filters the journal. Chips toggle: a
  second tag narrows again, to the entries carrying **every** chosen tag, and a chosen chip
  taps off. The filter lives in the address (`?tag=hike&tag=lake`), so it can be bookmarked and
  the back button undoes it. While filtered, a line says *3 moments with #hike + #lake* with
  *Show all*; tags no moment here carries together show an empty state with the way back. A
  chip in an entry's text filters by that one tag.
- **Visibility (§2.10) — a tag never widens access.** A tag is shown, offered and counted only
  over the moments the viewer may read. A tag carried only by private moments is seen by their
  author alone, marked with a lock (*Only you see this tag*); nobody else learns it exists.
  The lock, too, is read only from moments the viewer may read: two members who each use
  `#surprise` privately both keep it, and neither learns of the other's. Counts and the lock
  are worked out in the access layer's scoping (`childRecordVisibleTo`).
- **Found by search.** The full search finds a tag by its plain word like any other word of a
  moment, and a query that is one `#tag` finds exactly the moments carrying it (§2.9).
- **A page per tag.** `/tags/{key}` lists every moment the viewer may read that carries the
  tag, across everyone's journals, by day as on Home's stream: a big chip, *5 moments about 3
  people*, and *All tags*. A person's name opens their journal filtered by the tag, and a
  private moment is veiled as anywhere else. The moments are the stream's own read narrowed to
  the tag, so they are scoped exactly as Home's are; a tag nobody carries, and one only on
  another member's private moments, read the same: *No moments with #x*, with *All tags*.
- **All tags.** `/tags` lists every tag the viewer can see as chips with their counts, most
  used first, the lock on those only on their private moments. It has no place in the
  navigation; *All tags* on a tag's page and at the end of Home's Tags row lead there. With no
  tag yet it offers *Write a moment*, the composer opening with a `#` typed (`/?compose=tag`).
- **On Home's stream.** A third row of filter chips, *Tags*, follows *What* and *Who*: *All*,
  the eight most used tags and *All tags* (§2.22.2). A tag lives in the address
  (`?tag=hike`); choosing one narrows the stream to moments, choosing a second narrows again to
  the moments carrying both (`?tag=hike&tag=lake`), a chosen chip taps off, and choosing
  another kind clears them. While narrowed, a line says *5 moments with #hike* — *2 moments
  with #hike + #lake* — with *Show all*.


### 2.20.3 One moment about several people **[M3]**

*Specified 2026-11-08 (ADR-131); built 2026-11-09.* An evening with
the Brunners is one moment about Markus, Sandra, Lea and Tim — not four moments, and not one in
Markus's journal that the other three only find under *Mentioned in*.

- **Subjects, not mentions.** A moment is *about* one or more people, its **subjects**: it lands
  in each subject's journal and Activity. The first is the **anchor** (`contact_id`, as today);
  the others are stored beside it (`journal_subject`, `docs/03`). An `@` in the text stays a
  passive mention (§2.20.1): naming a subject with `@` renders their chip in the text but
  stores no mention, and *Mentioned in* never lists a subject — the subject rule
  (`mentionsOtherThan`) takes the whole list. Free text never makes anyone a subject.
- **Any people; a home is the shortcut.** Subjects are not bound to one home: grandma visiting
  or two families on a hike are the same thing. What the composer offers in one tap is the
  anchor's **home** — the people listed as *lives with …* (§2.2, `home_member`), only those the
  viewer sees; someone with two homes gets one offer per home. Moving out later changes nothing
  on old moments.
- **Written in the one composer.** On a person's page, under the anchor chip, and on Home once
  the first `@` has made an anchor, a quiet line offers *＋ Sandra, Lea & Tim · live with
  Markus* (*＋ Sandra, Lea & Tim · wohnen mit Markus*); a tap adds them all as chips, each
  removable. They are **offered, never preselected** — most moments are about one person.
  *＋ Person* adds anyone else. With more than one subject the anchor line reads *· goes to 4
  journals* (*· landet in 4 Tagebüchern*); the toast stays *Moment saved*. No new noun — not
  *household* (the tenant) and not *home* (the start page). `moment.capture` takes optional
  `subjectIds`; a moment kept on a phone before they existed has none and stays single-person.
  For a shared moment the offer and *＋ Person* hold only people the household sees; the rest
  of a home reads *Jonas only in a private moment*. A chip added while the moment was private
  greys with a lock once it is switched to *Shared*, and Save waits — it says why — until the
  chip is taken off or the moment is private again: dropping someone silently would lose them.
- **The slot is the set of people.** The day slot (§2.20) is keyed by the sorted subject ids
  (`subject_key`), author, day and visibility. A second moment for the *same* people that day
  joins the entry (ADR-086); one for a different set is its own — Markus's solo note and the
  family evening of the same day stay two entries. An edit that makes the people equal to
  another entry of the same author, day and visibility joins the two, as a merge does.
- **Read in every subject's place.** The entry appears in each subject's Activity (§2.23) and
  journal page, with *with Sandra, Lea and Tim* (*mit Sandra, Lea und Tim*) — the others, as
  linked names with small faces — under the kind line, and counts for each one's *last written
  about*. Home's stream shows **one** row (§2.22.2).
- **Counted once.** Search names all its people on the hit (§2.9); a tag page's *5 moments
  about 3 people* counts subjects (§2.20.2); *On this day* shows their faces (§2.12.1). One
  moment is one moment everywhere.
- **Edited by the author, from any subject's page.** The editor (§2.23, *Editing*) gains the
  chips row: text and people in one place; day, visibility and photos stay as today. Removing a
  chip takes the entry out of that person's Activity and journal and leaves it for the rest; if
  it was the anchor, the next subject becomes the anchor and the photos follow. The last chip
  cannot be removed — that is *Remove*. Only the author edits (`canEditAuthored`).
- **Removed for everyone.** *Remove* on any subject's page removes the one entry, with the
  usual undo window and admin rule (§2.23, `docs/03` §3.7); the toast names them: *Moment
  removed for Markus, Sandra, Lea and Tim*.
- **Photos stay on the one entry** and show under it on every subject's page; no copies.
- **One visibility.** The entry has one audience. A **shared** moment may be about
  household-visible people only — the mention rule (`allowedForAudience`); a **private** one
  about anyone the author sees. A private person in the home is offered only while the switch
  says *Private*, greyed with *only in a private moment* otherwise. So nobody reads a moment
  about a person they may not see.
- **Lifecycle.** Deleting a person removes them as a subject and re-anchors the entry; it goes
  only with its last subject. A merge moves subject rows to the survivor, dropping duplicates.
  Archiving changes nothing.
- **Archive and Monica.** The archive carries the subjects beside each entry (§2.15). Monica's
  journal entries name nobody and stay left out (§2.16); its multi-person activities stay
  touchpoints with participants.
