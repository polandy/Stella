## 2.18 Progressive Web App **[M2]**

Stella can be added to a phone's home screen and opened as its own window, and what has
already been read stays readable when it cannot be reached. Out of reach, a member can still
**add**: a moment is kept on the device and sent once Stella answers again (§2.18.1). Nothing
that already exists is edited offline, so nothing has to be merged (docs/04 §4.9, ADR-075);
how it is built is docs/04 §4.11.2.

**Installing.** A web app manifest and the icons the platforms ask for — 192 and 512, a
maskable 512 drawn small enough that a launcher cropping to a circle keeps all of it, and a
PNG for Safari, which reads no manifest. The manifest is a route rather than a file, because
the launcher name and description are strings a person reads and so come from the message
catalogue in their language (§2.19). The mark is re-laid on an opaque ground for this:
`static/logo.svg` is transparent and swaps palette with the system theme, neither of which
an icon may do.

**Opening it.** A cold start — the installed app launched from the home screen, or Stella in a
new tab, the sign-in page included — is greeted once per session: the logo's constellation
comes together, *Stella* rises under it, and the mark docks into the logo on the page, in about
1.4 s. A reload, navigation inside the app, coming back from the background and the sign-in
round trip show nothing. A tap or a key skips it, it holds back no loading, and under reduced
motion the finished mark stands for a moment instead (docs/05 §5.11.4).

**The offer to install** lives in **Settings → This device**, never over the app — an
install banner on a page somebody opened to read about their aunt is an interruption. It
says where things stand in each of the three states a device can be in: already installed,
installable (with the button), or a browser that offers no prompt, where it names the menu
item instead. Safari is the last of those.

**What a device keeps.** The build's own assets, every page as it is read — whether it was
loaded whole or reached by a link inside the app — and the photos in them. The places a phone
starts from — **Home, People, Circles and Settings** — are kept as soon as the app opens in
reach, so they open offline even when they were not read since the last update
(`KEPT_AHEAD`). So is **every person the member can see**: their page and their journal,
kept as the data a tap inside the app fetches, together with their avatar — the journal
because it is what is read before meeting someone (*what was it last time?*). Gallery and
moment photos are not fetched ahead, only kept when seen: they are the bulk of the bytes and
the least needed on a train. The list of who to keep (`GET /api/offline/people`) comes from the
same use-case as the People directory, and every page is the one the server renders for this
member, so another member's private data never reaches the device. They are brought up to
date in the background while Stella answers, one request at a time, at most every five
minutes (`REFRESH_INTERVAL_MS`), on any network. Each page is asked with a tag of the copy held,
so an unchanged one costs a few bytes (ADR-074, ADR-114). A person who is no longer visible
(deleted, merged, made private, archived, or given a new id by an archive import) leaves the
device on the next refresh, in every form a page was kept in: the whole page, its data and the
journal beneath it. An archived person is readable again once opened in reach. Nothing is
pruned on an answer that is not a list, which would otherwise empty the device. A pruned
person's avatar may stay in the photo cache, reachable from no page. A person's address opened
directly while out of reach shows the offline screen unless that page itself was read; inside
the app, every person is a tap away. Not the pages that describe the session (sign-in, sign-out, first-run setup), not the
health check, not a page reporting on an import or export run, and not a page carrying a
query string — a search is a question, not somewhere anyone returns to. Requests go to the
network first and fall back to the copy on the device: Stella is on the household's own
network, so the network is normally both reachable and the one telling the truth. A phone
that has lost its network does not always say so — a request can go out and never be answered
— so the network gets a few seconds (`NETWORK_PATIENCE_MS`) and silence counts as out of reach.
From then on a page with a copy is answered from the device at once, while the network is still
asked in the background; the first answer from it is what says Stella is back. A page
with a question in its address (`/?compose`) is answered, out of reach, by the kept page it
asks something of. A page that was never opened, asked for while out of reach, gets a plain
offline screen.

**Signing out empties the device.** A cached page is household data at rest on somebody's
phone, so the moment a session ends every cached page is thrown away. Note what this does
and does not cover: it protects a shared or handed-on device, and it does not encrypt
anything — a device left signed in holds the pages its owner has read, which is the same
bargain as the browser's own history.

**Saying so.** While Stella is out of reach, a line above the page says that what is showing
came off this device. It is not driven by `navigator.onLine`, which answers "is this device
on a network" — a phone on mobile data is perfectly online and cannot reach a Stella on the
household LAN at all, which is exactly the case this exists for. The service worker knows,
having just either fetched a page or failed to, so it reports and the page listens. A page
left open makes no request, so the moments the answer may have changed under it also ask the
worker to check: the phone losing or joining a network, and the app coming back into view. A
phone in flight mode shows the line at once; a phone whose network cannot reach Stella (VPN
off, away from home) shows it once the check has waited out the network's few seconds. When the
page came off the device, the line also says **how old it is** — *"Offline — from this
device, as of yesterday 18:04"* — read from the kept response's `Date` header: an old copy is
normal offline, but it must never look current. A refresh that finds the page unchanged
re-dates the copy, so the line says when the knowledge is from, not when the bytes first
arrived. The worker tracks this per window, for whole pages and page data only (a photo's age
is not the page's). There is no timer: a connection lost *while the app stays open on a
network that cannot reach Stella* is noticed on the next tap.

**What degrades.** A cached person page is the page as it was when last read, so anything
added since is not on it, and the parts that fetch on demand — the relationship map, search
— come up empty rather than stale. That is the intended trade: readable, and honest about
being a copy.

### 2.18.1 Keeping additions for later

The need is **adding**, not editing. The common way to be out of reach is a phone on mobile
data away from home — exactly when there is something to write down — and nobody needs to
rename a person or retype a relationship from a train. That split is what keeps offline small
(ADR-075).

**What counts as an addition.** Anything that adds a row: a moment (with the people created
inline in it), a person, a call or visit, a note, a relationship, a tag, a circle membership,
an entry on a person's journal page, a contact detail and a date (each its own row), and photos
for a person's or a circle's gallery. Setting something on a record that already exists is a
change, even when the field was empty — the birth date on a profile, a name, a profile picture,
pinning a favourite — and so is answering a suggestion or the household review. None of those
is kept.

While Stella is out of reach the composer's button says **Save for later**, and saving keeps
the moment on the device instead of sending it. The same happens when Stella stops answering in the middle of a save. A **note** written on a
person's page, a **call or visit** logged there, a **tag** or **circle**, a **contact detail**,
a **date**, **gallery photos** and a **relationship** added there, and an **entry on their
journal page** are kept the same way, and show where they will land: at the top of the
person's notes, Activity, relationships, photos or journal, or as a dashed chip beside their
tags, circles, contact details and dates. **Photos added to a circle** are kept the same way,
above the circle's *Photos* grid, with the role and visibility chosen when they were added
(with the 1600 px view beside each large picture). Cutting a profile picture out of a group
photo is a change, not an addition, so it is not kept: it is sent at once or it fails. So is
changing a circle photo's role, pin or visibility, as with a person's pins. A relationship is checked against the guardrails (§2.4) when it arrives, so one that
has meanwhile become a duplicate or a contradiction comes back as *Could not send*.
- **Where it shows.** A kept moment shows under the capture field as *Not sent yet* — not at
  the top of the stream, where on a phone the rail above it would push it out of sight. Home
  lists every kept item, whoever it is about, with the name stored when it was kept (`about`),
  since nothing can be looked up offline. Kept items look unlike sent ones (a dashed edge,
  *Could not send* in the danger colour with the reason), so nobody takes them for household
  knowledge others can already see.
- **Editing what has not been sent.** Nobody else has seen it, so changing it cannot conflict.
  Moments, notes and calls reopen in their own form, which saves into the kept copy: the edit
  replaces what is kept rather than queuing a change behind it, so the device only ever sends
  additions — a person kept offline and then given a birthday is one `contact.add` with the
  birthday in it. Tags, circles, relationships, journal-page entries, contact details, dates
  and gallery photos offer *Discard* only: they are quicker typed again than edited in place
  (ADR-109). While an item is on its way it is locked, so an edit cannot land between Stella
  storing it and the device hearing so; a send that fails for want of a connection unlocks it.
- **Discarding** asks twice, inline — *This device holds the only copy.* → *Discard for good*
  — rather than offering an undo, because there is no server copy for an undo to fall back on.
  A chip discards in one click: what is lost is one word.
- **How a kept moment reads.** Its text as typed (`@LenaBrunner`), not with names resolved; a
  photo *count* rather than thumbnails; and a moment sent later offers no *"Link …?"* hint.
- **The composer out of reach.** On a phone the *What happened?* sheet opens without a round
  trip (SvelteKit shallow routing), so the pencil works offline; from another page it links to
  `/?compose`, which the worker answers with the kept Home page. Its default day is the
  device's own date when that is later than the day the cached page was rendered, or a page
  kept since yesterday would date today's moment yesterday.
- **When it is sent.** It is sent without being asked: when the app opens, when Stella answers
  again, when the app comes back into view, and when the phone joins a network. Nothing polls.
- **On arrival.** Stella checks it exactly as if it had been written there and then. A moment
  it refuses — someone mentioned was deleted in the meantime, say — stays as *Could not send*
  with the reason, to be corrected or discarded, never dropped.
- **One path, in reach or not.** Every one of these saves goes through the same keeping and
  sending; in reach it is simply sent at once and the form waits for the answer, so a save
  looks as it always did and a refusal shows in the form, with what was typed still there. Only
  a save whose answer never comes is left kept; *never* is ten seconds, a photo's a minute.
  While the form waits, the item is not listed as kept, so a save that goes through never
  flashes up as *Not sent yet* (ADR-076).
- **A refused save leaves the device at once.** The form still holds what was typed, with the
  reason beside it, and correcting it saves as a new command; keeping it as *Could not send*
  as well would show the same text twice (ADR-110).
- **Sent once.** Every moment carries a name made when it was written, and Stella remembers
  every name it has applied, so a moment whose answer was lost on the way is recognised
  rather than saved twice (`command_receipt`, docs/03 §3.3).
- **Whose it is.** Kept moments belong to the member who wrote them. Another member signing
  in on the same device neither sees nor sends them. Signing out with moments still waiting
  asks first — *Keep and sign out* (they are sent after the member's next sign-in) or
  *Discard and sign out* — because it must never take the only copy along unasked.
- **Photos too.** A moment's photos are processed in the browser as always (downscaled,
  location stripped) and kept with it. They are sent after the moment has arrived, each on its
  own, so a photo that fails keeps only itself waiting; the kept moment says *Photos not sent
  yet* until the last one is in. Once Stella has the moment it is household data, and the
  kept copy can no longer be edited, only discarded. A photo refused after its entry went in
  does not undo the save: the form closes, and only that photo stays as *Could not send*
  (ADR-111).
- **A new person** added on *Add person* is kept too. Their page cannot open before Stella has
  them, so the form says they are kept and stays ready for the next one; they show on Home as
  not sent yet. Linking them to a relative found by the duplicate check (§2.2.1) needs Stella,
  so that offer is not made out of reach.

### 2.18.2 What can go wrong on arrival

Every kept item is checked again when it arrives, with the rules of that moment — never trusted
because it was valid when written. The rule behind the table: **the outbox is the only copy of
what the member wrote**, so nothing leaves it until Stella has confirmed it or the member
discards it.

| On arrival | Result |
|---|---|
| everything still valid | applied, and gone from the device |
| someone it names was deleted, merged or made invisible to the author | *Could not send*, with the reason; to be corrected or discarded, never dropped |
| a relationship the guardrails (§2.4) now refuse — someone linked the two meanwhile | *Could not send*, with the guardrail's reason |
| the session has ended (an OIDC sign-out, say) | waits, and is sent after signing in again |
| the same item arrives twice — the connection dropped after Stella applied it | recognised by its receipt and answered *already applied*, however late |
| the person's journal already has an entry that day, from any device | added to it, never replacing it (§2.22.1, §2.20) |
| something on Stella's side failed | waits and is tried again; Stella logs it |

Every check an addition can meet answers with a sentence the member can read, so a refusal is
something to act on rather than a command the phone would retry for ever (ADR-112). Items are
sent oldest first, because a later one may name something an earlier one created.
