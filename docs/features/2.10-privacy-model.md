## 2.10 Privacy model (shared vs. private) **[M1]**

- Every shareable record (**contact, note, photo, interaction**) has a **visibility**:
  `shared` (whole household) or `private` (only the creator).
- **Shared by default:** every new record starts `shared`. Stella is open by design: the
  household sees what its members add, and *private* is the exception, chosen per record.
  There is deliberately no per-member preference that turns this around.
- **Inheritance & rules:**
  - A **private contact** and everything under it are visible only to its creator,
    regardless of child visibility.
  - A **shared contact** may still have **private notes/photos/interactions** —
    visible only to their author.
  - **Relationships** are visible if the viewer can see **both** endpoints; a
    relationship touching a private contact they don't own is hidden from them.
- Changing a contact from shared → private warns about the effect on shared children.
- A record's author can change its visibility afterwards where its editor offers the choice:
  a circle's photo (*Make private*, 2.14) and a note (2.5).
- Admins do **not** gain access to others' private records (see 2.1). The one thing an admin
  may do to another member's record is **remove** it, and only a **shared** note (2.5,
  docs/03 §3.7); never edit it.


### 2.10.1 Private entries veiled on screen **[M3]**

A private entry's content stays blurred on its author's own screen until they ask for it, so a
glance over the shoulder, or a phone handed round to show a photo, shows *that* something
private is there, never *what*. It is **not access control**: the access layer (docs/03 §3.7)
already keeps everyone else out, and the private text is in the page. The threat is a glance,
not someone holding the unlocked phone.

- **What is veiled:** the **title, text and picture** of every private **note, moment
  (journal entry), touchpoint and photo**, wherever it shows: the person page's notes, story
  and photos, the journal page, the home stream, both photo lightboxes, *Mentioned in* and
  search. The **kind, day, author and the *Private* mark stay readable**, so the story still
  reads in order and it is clear where to tap.
- **Not veiled:** a private person's or circle's **name** (it is how they are found; their
  page's private entries are veiled like any other), gift rows, everything shared, the command
  palette (it lists people and actions, never an entry's words), and a **kept** item waiting
  in the offline outbox (§2.18): that is what the member has just written.
- **Revealing:**
  - **With device unlock set up** on this device, tapping a veil asks for the device's own
    fingerprint, face or PIN (a WebAuthn passkey). Confirmed, **every** private entry on every
    page shows for **five minutes**; a cancel or a failure leaves everything blurred and says
    so in a toast.
  - **Without it**, or in a browser that cannot do it (no WebAuthn, or not a secure context
    such as plain http), **a tap shows that one entry** until the page is left; a **Hide**
    beside its mark blurs it again.
  - **The header lock** (docs/05 §5.4) counts the blurred entries on the page (*3 private*)
    and shows them all for five minutes, after the device check where there is one; while
    open it reads *Private shown · 4:59*, and a tap blurs everything again. It is not there
    when the page holds nothing blurred.
- **It locks itself:** Stella going to the background (the tab hidden, the phone locked, the
  app switched) blurs everything again at once, whatever was shown. The window lives in the
  tab's memory only: a reload starts blurred.
- **Editing shows it:** opening the editor on a private note, moment or touchpoint reveals
  it; the author is writing it anyway. The composer never veils what is being typed.
- **Inside a link** (a mention, a search hit) the snippet is blurred with no button of its
  own: the link opens the entry, and the header lock shows it in place.
- **Search still finds private text**, it is the author's own search; only the snippet is
  blurred.
- **Accessibility:** while veiled the words are **not on the page**. A stand-in of the same
  shape (`placeholderFor` in `src/lib/privacy/veil.ts`, the word lengths and line breaks
  kept) is blurred instead, so find-in-page cannot land on them, and it is `aria-hidden`,
  inert and unselectable. The veil is a real button announced *Private note, hidden. Show*.
  A photo is blurred itself. The unveil is the shared fade (docs/05 §5.11) and follows
  reduced motion.
- **Offline:** the veil, the window and the device check are all on the device, so they work
  offline unchanged.
- **The switch:** *Blur my private entries* in Settings · Privacy (§2.17), the member's own,
  **on by default** (`user.blur_private`, docs/03). Off, private entries read like any other
  for that member, on every device. A display preference, not a default visibility: *shared by
  default* (above) is untouched.
- **Device unlock** is per device and per member, set up in Settings · Privacy:
  - *Set up device unlock* opens the device's own passkey prompt
    (`navigator.credentials.create`, a platform authenticator, `userVerification: 'required'`,
    the member's id as the user handle). The credential id and the day are kept in this
    browser's `localStorage`: being set up is a fact about this device. Another member signing
    in on it has not set it up.
  - Before a reveal: `navigator.credentials.get` for that one credential with a fresh random
    challenge and `userVerification: 'required'`.
  - *Forget on this device…* drops the stored id, after a confirm step. The passkey itself
    stays in the device's password manager, where the member may delete it; other devices
    keep theirs.
- **Why checked on the device, not the server:** sending private text only after a fresh
  server-verified assertion would be real protection, but private entries could then not be
  read offline (§2.18), every page holding one would need a second request, and Stella would
  need a passkey table and verify endpoints beside its sign-in. The aim is the glance, which
  the device check covers.
