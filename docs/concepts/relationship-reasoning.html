Stella · concept · relationship suggestions

Why Stella thinks so

A suggestion currently shows one fact underneath it — and never the one that names the person being offered. This is what the row would say instead, and what happens when every name in it becomes a way to that person.

The row today

Stella offers Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier because Otto is already Fabio's parent and Fabio and Lisa are siblings. Two facts. The line under the claim shows one of them, and it is the one that never mentions Otto — so the reader is told a true thing that does not answer the question they asked.

LM Lisa Meier
  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN Fabio Meier is Lisa Meier’s sibling. L1

L1 is the rule's name in the engine. It is in the markup today, and it means nothing to anyone who did not write it.

Three ways to say why

All three carry the whole chain and drop the rule id. They differ in how much room they take on a screen that is built to fold — ten people to a page, five claims each.

A · one sentence

  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN Otto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier, and Fabio Meier and Lisa Meier are siblings.

height: —

B · the chain, stacked

  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN on recordOtto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier on recordFabio Meier and Lisa Meier are siblings

height: —

C · folded behind “Why?”

  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN
    Why this is offered
    Otto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier, and Fabio Meier and Lisa Meier are siblings.

height: —

D · one sentence, tightened

  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN Parent of Fabio Meier, who is Lisa Meier’s sibling.

height: —

E · two premises, no labels

  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN Otto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier Fabio Meier and Lisa Meier are siblings

height: —

A — the whole chain, in full names. At the review’s own column width it costs the row nothing: 66px, exactly what stands there today, because the sentence fits the line the reason already occupies. On a phone it is the dearest of the readable options — 128px against today’s 84px — and D’s tightened wording buys 19px of that back by leaving the offered person out of the sentence and naming two people instead of three. B and E spend a whole extra line even on a desktop (86px), and B’s on record labels repeat what certain already said. C is free only because it answers nothing until it is tapped — and this is the one question the reader has.

Every name, a way to that person

Names appear in three places on this screen: the claim, the reason, and the person the block is about. The third is a link already. Two ways to treat the other two — the question is whether a review row turns into a field of blue.

Underlined, in the text's own colour

  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN Otto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier, and Fabio Meier and Lisa Meier are siblings.
  • Otto Meier is a parent of Selina Meier CERTAIN Otto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier, and Fabio Meier and Selina Meier are siblings.

The row still reads as a sentence; the underline says it can be followed.

Link colour

  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN Otto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier, and Fabio Meier and Lisa Meier are siblings.
  • Otto Meier is a parent of Selina Meier CERTAIN Otto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier, and Fabio Meier and Selina Meier are siblings.

Six links to a row, eight per person, up to eighty on a page.

Underlined, in the text's own colour. A name is not a call to action here — the two buttons are. The underline is enough to say it can be followed, and it keeps the claim readable as the sentence it is.

German, which is where a spliced sentence breaks

The reason is not built by gluing pieces together: German puts the same three names in a different order, and the genitive the English sentence uses does not exist in it. The sentence is written whole in each language, and the names are placed into it wherever that language puts them.

EN

  • Otto Meier is a parent of Lisa Meier CERTAIN Otto Meier is a parent of Fabio Meier, and Fabio Meier and Lisa Meier are siblings.

DE

  • Otto Meier ist ein Elternteil von Lisa Meier SICHER Otto Meier ist ein Elternteil von Fabio Meier, und Fabio Meier und Lisa Meier sind Geschwister.

Same three people, six links, two languages. English says is a parent of where German says ist ein Elternteil von, and the two halves of the sentence do not sit in the same order — so whatever builds this hands the finished sentence its names, rather than assembling the sentence out of pieces. That is also why a name inside the reason can be a link at all: the sentence knows where each person landed in it.

Both languages were measured in the column the review really uses (640px of row inside a 672px panel), and again at 390px. The German sentence is the longer one — it needs the dative apposition where English uses a genitive — and on a desktop it still lands on the same line.

There is no German penalty on a desktop — 66px, the same as English. An earlier draft of this page reported 105px and argued for shortening the sentence; that number came from this mockup’s own comparison cards, which were narrower than the column the review actually renders in. Measured at the real width, full names cost nothing. The phone is where German is dearer: 149px against 84px today, because three names and two premises need three lines at 390px. That is the price of answering the question, and the fold is what keeps the page finite regardless.
