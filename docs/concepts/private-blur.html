Concept · private entries veiled on screen

Your private entries stay blurred until you ask for them

A private note, moment, touchpoint or photo is already hidden from the rest of the household. This adds a guard for its own author: someone looking over your shoulder, or a phone handed round at the table, sees that something private is there, never what it says. Pick a reveal variant and try it; the phone and the desktop share one state.

What happens

  1. Blurred, not hidden. A private entry keeps its kind, day, author and the Private mark; its title, text and photo are blurred. You see that it is there and where, so the story still reads in order.
  2. Tap to show. Tapping the veil shows that one entry. Hide blurs it again; so does leaving the page or Stella going to the background.
  3. Tap one, or show all. Tapping shows that one entry. The lock in the header shows every private entry, on every page, for five minutes; it then reads Private shown · 4:59 and a tap locks again.
  4. Fingerprint first. Any reveal asks the phone's own unlock (passkey: fingerprint, face or PIN). Then everything private is shown for five minutes. Needs one passkey per device, set up in Settings.
  5. Locks itself. Stella going to the background always blurs everything again, at once; nothing stays open for whoever picks the phone up next.
  6. Editing is yours. Opening the editor on a private note shows it; you are writing it anyway.
  7. Screen readers hear Private note, hidden. Show instead of the blurred text, and the text is not selectable or findable with Ctrl+F while veiled.
Honest limit of CThe fingerprint guards the screen, not the data: the page already holds the text, and it works offline only because the check stays on the device. Making it real protection means the server sends private text only after a fresh unlock, and then a private note can't be read offline.
