Concept · private entries veiled on screen

Your private entries stay blurred until you ask for them

A private note, moment, touchpoint or photo is already hidden from the rest of the household. This adds a guard for its own author: someone looking over your shoulder, or a phone handed round at the table, sees that something private is there, never what it says. Decided: the device's own unlock (passkey) shows them; a device without one falls back to a tap. Settings holds the switch and the set-up. Phone and desktop share one state.

Person page

  1. Blurred, not hidden. A private entry keeps its kind, day, author and the Private mark; its title, text and photo are blurred. You see that it is there and where, so the story still reads in order.
  2. Fingerprint first. Tapping a veil, or the 3 private lock in the header, asks the phone’s own unlock (fingerprint, face or PIN). Then every private entry is shown for five minutes; the lock reads Private shown · 4:59, and a tap locks again.
  3. No passkey here: a tap. Tapping shows that one entry until you leave the page; Hide blurs it again. The header lock shows every private entry for five minutes, without a prompt.
  4. Locks itself. Stella going to the background always blurs everything again, at once.
  5. Editing is yours. Opening the editor on a private note shows it; you are writing it anyway.
  6. Screen readers hear Private note, hidden. Show; the veiled text is not selectable or findable with Ctrl+F.

Settings · Privacy (new)

  1. One section, after You. It is about you and this device, not the household.
  2. The switch Blur my private entries is on by default and belongs to the member: off, private entries read like any other on every device, and the device row goes away.
  3. Device unlock is per device. Set up device unlock opens the phone’s own passkey prompt; after that the row says where and since when. Each phone or computer is set up on its own.
  4. Forget on this device asks first, in the sunken confirm step (focus on Keep it). The passkey itself stays in the phone’s password manager; Stella only stops asking for it here.
  5. A browser that can’t (or the http preview) says so and keeps the tap.
