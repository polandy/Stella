Stella · concept · relationship review

The row that leaves

An answered suggestion should go at once, softly — not sit there for eight seconds wearing a tick. The catch is what going costs: every row below it moves up by its height, and the next one lands under the finger that was about to tap. Each proposal below is live. Answer a row and watch the readout: it measures where the next row was, and where it is.

Why it stands there today

The row settles in place and keeps its shape for the undo window. That came out of a measurement: a row that changed shape the instant it was answered shortened the list by 65px and moved the page under the reader's next tap. Holding the shape fixed it — at the price of a list that fills up with answered rows nobody wants to look at any more.

What is not at stake: the undo window itself. The accept or the decline is still held eight seconds and still sent only when the window closes, so Undo still prevents a write instead of reversing one. This is only about what the list does meanwhile.

Four proposals

All four make the row leave immediately. They differ in what happens to the rows below it.

D — the row is gone in 120ms, and nothing below it moves while a hand is still over the list. It is the only one that also holds at the top of the list, where there is no scroll to give back and B and C shift the next row by a full 74px like A does. What it costs is a blank gap where the row was, for as long as the reader keeps pointing at that spot — then the gaps close together, softly. Take B instead if that gap reads as a hole: it is identical everywhere except at the very top. A is what “just make it disappear” gives you on its own — 74px under the finger, on every answer, wherever the reader is.

What the readout measures

Not opinions about smoothness: the position of the next row, in the viewport, before the answer and after the animation has finished. Zero means nothing moved under the reader. Answer a few rows in each proposal — scrolled down as well as at the top, since that is where the difference shows.

Driven in the pinned Playwright container, not judged by eye: each proposal answers a row with the list at the top and again scrolled, and the position of the same row below it is read before and after. D’s gap, when it finally closes, moves that row by 74px like everything else — but by then the reader has left the spot, which is the whole point. Under prefers-reduced-motion every variant skips the animation and the row simply goes.

Two of these numbers were wrong the first time I took them, in ways only a second look caught: a click below the fold scrolled the page and read as the list moving, and a locator that skipped answered rows handed back a different row afterwards. Both made a still list look like a jumping one.
