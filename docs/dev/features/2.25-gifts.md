## 2.25 Gift ideas and gifts given **[M3]**

Two things are hard to remember and annoying to get wrong: what the household gave whom, and
when — so the same book does not arrive twice — and a good idea the moment it comes, so it is
there on the birthday it was meant for. Decided with the maintainer on 2026-10-06 and built in
three slices (#316, #329, and the hints and the palette entry of §2.25.5).

### 2.25.1 What a gift is

A gift belongs to **one person**, the one it is for (a couple's present is noted on each).

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | a short title, required: *Teapot, cast iron* |
| **Note** | optional: size, colour, where they saw it |
| **Link** | optional web address — the shop page. Only `http(s)` is kept; an address pasted without its scheme is read as `https://`; anything else (`javascript:`, `mailto:`) is refused |
| **State** | **idea**, **bought** (§2.25.9), **given**, or **received** (a present from them to the household) |
| **On** | the day a given or received gift changed hands; an idea has none |
| **Occasion** | optional: *Birthday*, *Christmas*, *Anniversary* — stored as a key and shown in the reader's language — or free text, shown as typed |
| **When** | an open gift only, idea or bought: *now* (the default), from an age, for a birthday, for Christmas at an age, for a day, or *later* — §2.25.8 |
| **Visibility** | shared by default, private per record (§2.10), or *everyone but* the person it is for (§2.25.10) |
| **Giver** | optional, not on a received gift: a person, or the household itself (*Wir*) — §2.25.12 |

An idea and the gift it became are **the same record**: *Mark as given* sets the state and asks
only for the day and the occasion; title, note and link stay. A received gift is noted directly,
never from an idea. Who noted it is kept and shown as *noted by*; who gives it is the giver
(§2.25.12) — nobody named means *us*, as before there were givers.

**Who may change what.** Whoever may see a gift may rewrite it, mark it given and remove it —
a present is the household's, and the member who buys the teapot is rarely the one who noted
it. Only its author may make it private, shared again, or kept from the person it is for
(§2.25.10), so nobody can hide a gift from the member who wrote it down.

### 2.25.2 The Gifts card

On the person page, after *Notes* and before *Mentioned in* (`'gifts'` in `CONTACT_SECTIONS`),
with its own jump-bar link.

- **With nothing noted** the card is one line (docs/dev/05 §5.5): *Gifts · No gift ideas yet.*,
  *+ Idea*, and the ⋯ menu holding *+ Given* and *+ Received*.
- **Tabs:** *Ideas* (open ideas, newest first; the tab carries their count, *Ideas · 3*, and
  the ideas still waiting for their time fold beneath under *Later · n*, §2.25.8),
  *Given* (newest day first, under uppercase year dividers) and *Received* (the same — the tab
  shows only once there is one). An empty Ideas tab invites: *Got an idea for Hilde? Note it
  here, and it is here when the day comes.* The card header has no count, so its jump-bar link
  has none either.
- **Adding:** *+ Idea* asks for the title; note and link fold away behind *Note or link*.
  *+ Given* (beside it) and *+ Received* (in the ⋯ menu) ask for the day (today by default)
  and the occasion as well. Saving needs only the title, and the day where there is one.
- **Already given.** While the title is typed — adding an idea or a given gift, or rewriting
  one — a title that looks like a gift already **given** to this person says so under the
  field: *Already given: “Teapot, cast iron” on 12 October 2023 (Birthday).* It looks like one
  when either title holds the other, case and accents folded (*teekanne* finds *Teekanne aus
  Gusseisen*), from three letters on; of several, the latest is named; the gift being rewritten
  never finds itself. Ideas and received gifts are not looked at, and nothing is asked of
  Stella — the card already holds every gift the reader may see (`alreadyGiven`,
  `src/lib/gifts/already-given.ts`). It is a hint, not a block: the second tin of her favourite
  tea can be on purpose.
- **Each row:** the title, its day and occasion, the note, *noted by …*, the link, *private*.
  An idea has no day of its own, so it says when it was noted instead — *Added 3 October 2026*,
  from the record's creation time, as the reader's calendar day. An idea offers *Mark as given…*, which opens the day and the occasion; every gift can be rewritten in place (pencil) and removed with
  the undo window every removal has (§2.23).
- **Offline:** noting a gift is a command (`gift.add`) and goes through the outbox like every
  addition (§2.18, docs/dev/04 §4.11.2) — kept on the device and shown on the card until sent.
  Rewriting, giving, answering *Still current?* and removing (`gift.edit`, `gift.markGiven`,
  `gift.answerWhen`, `gift.remove`) change what others may have seen, so they wait for Stella.

### 2.25.3 In the story

A given or received gift appears in the person's story (*Activity*, §2.23) on its day,
labelled *Given* or *Received*, with the gift icon and `--kind-gift`, its title linking to the
Gifts card and its occasion below. It is **read from the gift record**, a third source of the
merge, so editing or removing the gift changes the story too. Ideas never appear there, and the
story offers no removal for a gift — that happens on the card.

There is no touchpoint kind *Gift* any more (§2.25.4): the gift icon and `--kind-gift` belong
to the gift record alone, and *Log contact* offers *+ Given* on the Gifts card in its place.

### 2.25.4 Gifts from before

Before the gift record, gifts lived in two other shapes. Both become gift records once, so there
is one place for gifts:

- **The Monica gift notes.** The import wrote each Monica gift as a note titled *Gift* /
  *Geschenk* (id `<source>:gift:<monica id>`), its first line `🎁 **Title** — status, day`, then
  the comment and the link. Such a note becomes a gift: Monica's `idea` an idea, `offered`
  given, `received` received. The title, day, comment (as the note) and link stay — the link
  only when it is a web address, read the way every gift link is (§2.25.1); otherwise it stays
  in the note. The visibility, author and the note's own creation time stay too, and the gift
  keeps the note's id. Then the note goes.
- **The gift touchpoints.** Each touchpoint of the kind *gift* becomes a **given** gift on its
  person, on its day, keeping its visibility, author and creation time. Its title becomes the
  gift's title and its description the gift's note; with no title the description is the
  title, with neither the gift is called *Gift* / *Geschenk* in its author's language. A
  touchpoint with participants becomes one gift per person (a gift belongs to one person). Then
  the touchpoint goes, and the kind with it.
- **Nothing written by hand is lost.** A gift note somebody edited since the import, one whose
  first line no longer reads as the import wrote it, and one Stella cannot make a gift of
  (given or received without a day, a status Monica does not have) is left exactly as it is and
  named in the server log (docs/dev/07 §7.10). An original is removed only together with the gifts
  made from it, in one transaction.
- **When.** At every start, after the database migrations, and after every restore. Once nothing
  is left to convert, it writes nothing; the notes it leaves alone are named again at each start.
  Re-running is harmless: a gift takes its original's id (a participant's gift
  `<touchpoint id>:<person id>`), so an original that comes back — from an older archive, say —
  finds its gift already there and is only removed.
- **The story** shows the converted gifts on their old days, as *Given* or *Received* (§2.25.3);
  ideas never appear there.
- **Search.** A gift that was a note leaves the note index and is found as a gift instead, by
  its title and note, on the full search page (§2.9, §2.25.5).
- **The importer** writes Monica's gifts as gift records from now on, under the same ids. One it
  cannot make a gift of stays the note it always was; one whose earlier note was kept because
  somebody wrote in it is not written again.
- **Restore.** An older archive that still holds gift notes or gift touchpoints restores them as
  they are and the same conversion runs straight after, so it lands as gift records; the
  restore's report says how many gifts it made (§2.15).

### 2.25.5 Before an occasion, and from anywhere

- **Coming up.** A person in Home's *Coming up* band (§2.13.3) with open ideas the reader may
  see shows a third line under their row: the gift icon and *2 ideas*, a link to their Gifts
  card. Nothing new is sent or scheduled; the hint only stands where the occasion already does.
  The counts are one scoped query for the people in the band (`countOpenIdeas`).
- **The command palette** (docs/dev/05 §5.4) offers *Gift idea for …* next to *Write a moment*.
  It does not leave the palette: the field empties and asks *Gift idea for whom?*, the list
  becomes the people alone, and Enter on one lands on their page with the Gifts card's idea
  form open and the cursor in *What?* (`?gift=idea`). Backspace in the empty
  field, or the back arrow in front of it, returns to the palette's start. The idea is saved
  like any other — through the outbox when Stella is out of reach.
- **The search page** (§2.9) finds a gift by its title or its note, in a group of its own, and
  each hit leads to the person's Gifts card. ⌘K and Home's search field do not look at gifts.

### 2.25.6 Every open idea of the household

The *Christmas shopping list*: **`/gifts`** shows every open idea the reader may see, so the
member doing the shopping need not open each person. Decided with the maintainer on 2026-10-30.

- **By person**, one card each: avatar, name (a link to their Gifts card), how many ideas, and
  their **next occasion** — their next birthday, or Christmas Eve (24 December) when that comes
  first or no birthday is known: *Birthday in 3 days · 2 Nov*, *Christmas in 55 days · 24 Dec*.
  Whoever's occasion comes first is on top; people on the same day by name. An occasion 30 days
  off or closer is tinted. Ideas within a card newest first.
- **By occasion** (2026-11-21): under the heading a two-way tab list, *By person* · *By
  occasion*, kept per member in the browser like the waiting choice. *By occasion* lists the days
  soonest first (*Christmas 2026 · in 33 days · 24 Dec*), and under each **every recipient**, a
  child or an adult, as a block: who gives what (*Sandra is giving it*, *We are giving it*), the
  free ideas (*still free*) and the done or bought ones dimmed last, with the card's tally
  (§2.25.13) per recipient and per day. Unlike *By person* it holds bought gifts and ideas that still
  wait for their day, because the question is whether everyone is covered. A gift with no day
  comes last under *Without an occasion* and only once somebody gives it or it is bought. A day
  that went by says *… is over. Record what was given…* and opens *Nachtragen…* on it (§2.25.14).
  The view only reads: a gift is changed on its person's card, which the recipient's name opens.
  Each idea on *By person* also names its giver.
- **Each idea** as on the card: title, note, *noted by …*, link, *private*. *Mark as given…*
  opens the day (today) and the occasion, preset to the person's next occasion. The idea leaves
  the list at once, and the toast offers *Undo* for the same few seconds a removal has (§2.23);
  only then is `gift.markGiven` sent. It needs Stella, as on the card.
- **Ideas for later** are folded away (§2.25.8): *Show 3 ideas for later* under the heading
  brings them in, after the person's other ideas and dimmed; *Hide the ideas for later* folds
  them again. Each member's choice is kept in their browser, per member, so a shared tablet
  keeps two. The count beside the heading stays the open ones.
- **What is left out:** given and received gifts, and the ideas of an archived person (they
  stay on that person's page). Visibility is the central rule (§2.10): a private idea only for
  its author.
- **No nudges.** People without ideas do not appear — no *coming up, no idea yet* row; *Coming
  up* on Home already says whose day is near.
- **Reached from** the command palette's *All gift ideas* and the Gifts card's ⋯ menu. It has no
  place in the navigation (docs/dev/05 §5.5.13).

### 2.25.7 Not planned

Prices and budgets, wish lists the person shares themselves, and email reminders.

### 2.25.8 An idea for later

A good idea often comes years early: a bike with gears *from age 8*, a telescope *for the 10th
birthday*, a toy train *for Christmas at 6*, a snow globe *for Christmas 2027*. Noted now, such an idea would sit among the open
ones for years and wear the list down. Decided with the maintainer on 2026-11-17: the time is
captured, the idea kept out of the way until then, and it comes back by itself when it is due.

- **When it is for**, chosen on the idea form as chips: **Now** (the default — open at once),
  **From age N**, **For the Nth birthday**, **Christmas at age N**, **On a day** (with
  *Christmas YYYY* quick picks for this year while it is still ahead and for the next), and
  **Later**. An age has a stepper
  starting one year above their current age. One plain line under the chips says what the choice
  comes to — *Turns 10 on 14 March 2028. It waits until 15 December 2027, then shows among the
  ideas.* — or why it cannot be kept.
- **Christmas at age N** is the Christmas Eve on which they **are** N (ADR-135; owner decision
  2026-11-18): the year they turn N, or the year after for a birthday from 25 to 31 December —
  born 28 December 2023, they are still 5 on Christmas Eve 2029, so *Christmas at 6* is 24
  December 2030. Born on Christmas Eve, it is the Nth birthday itself. An estimated or bare year
  gives *about* Christmas of the year they turn N. The line under the chips names it — *Christmas
  2028, at age 6.* — and the chip on the row reads *Christmas at 6*.
- **Waiting.** An idea for an age or a day waits until **90 days before** its day — the birthday
  on which they turn N, that Christmas Eve, or the day itself. A waiting idea counts nowhere: not in the *Ideas* tab,
  not on Home's *Coming up*, not in the brief, and on `/gifts` it is folded away unless the
  member asks for it (§2.25.6). On the person's card it stays in
  reach, folded under *Later · n*, soonest first and *later* last, each row with its chip — *From
  age 8*, *For the 10th birthday*, *Christmas at 6*, *Christmas 2027*, *Later*. With nothing open the tab says
  *Nothing due yet. The ideas for later wait below.*
- **Soon, then due.** From 90 days before its day the idea leaves the fold and counts again like
  any open idea — in the tab, on Home, in the brief and on `/gifts` — its chip saying how near:
  *For the 6th birthday · in 34 days*, *· tomorrow*, *· today* (*· about 2030* for an estimated
  year). From its day on it is due and the chip names only the time. *From an age* stays due
  for good: once they are 8, a bike *from age 8* never goes stale.
- **Occasion passed.** A birthday or a day that went by without the idea being given leaves it
  open and marked: the chip says *· passed* and the row asks *The occasion has passed. Still
  current?* Nothing is removed on its own. The answers: **Keep as idea** (it becomes a plain one,
  for now), **New time** (the picker, checked against today like any other), or **Remove** (with
  the undo window, §2.23). Until somebody answers, it stays where it is, still asking. An
  estimated year has passed only once its whole year is over.
- **The yearly look at *Later*.** An idea for *later* is looked at once a year, from 90 days
  before the person's birthday — or, for someone without a birthday's day and month, before the
  anniversary of the day it was noted. Then it leaves the fold, counts again and asks *Noted for
  later. Still current?* with the same three answers (*New time* can choose *Later* again). Once
  answered — or once its time is chosen anew in the edit form — it is not asked about again
  until next year's look.
- **Derived, never stored.** Where an idea stands is worked out from its time, the person's
  birthday and the household's day (`whenState`, `src/lib/when/state.ts`); the only thing kept
  is the day of the last answer (`when_checked_on`, docs/dev/03), so *later* knows its look is
  done. The command `gift.answerWhen` carries the answer (docs/dev/04 §4.11.2); like rewriting
  it waits for Stella.
- **An age needs a birth year.** Without one the age chips stay shut and the picker links to
  *Estimate age*, the person's dates editor (§2.13.1). An estimated year names no day, so the
  age is counted from 1 January of its year and said as *about* — *Turns 12 in about 2030*.
  Should the birth year later be removed, an idea for an age no longer waits: it is never lost
  unseen. A 29 February birthday falls on 1 March in a common year, as on Home. The idea keeps
  the age, never a worked-out day, so a birthday corrected later moves it along.
- **What is refused.** A birthday already over, a Christmas at an age already over, a day
  already past, an age outside 1–150. *From
  an age* they have already reached simply holds now and is kept as *now*.
- **Rewriting** an idea keeps its time unless another is chosen; a chosen one is checked again
  against today. *Mark as given* clears it — a given gift waits for nothing. The age and the day
  live in `src/lib/when/`, pure and shared: an outing idea (§2.27) reuses the same rules and
  the same picker (`WhenPicker.svelte`), with its own forms — *in a month* (`in_month`) among
  them, which a gift does not offer.
- **In the calendar link** (§2.13.4), once soon or due: a one-off all-day event on its day —
  the birthday on which they turn N, that Christmas Eve, or the day itself — *Gift idea: Bike (Mia)*, its chip as
  the description, linked to the person's page. It needs a day: an estimated birth year, an idea
  for *later*, one that is *now* or whose occasion passed stay out. So does any idea on the
  member's own record, so a calendar never spoils a surprise. Visibility is the feed's: a
  private idea only in its author's, an archived or private person's as on `/gifts`. A group's
  ideas on one day are one event naming everyone (§2.25.11).
- **Not yet:** per-item visibility of the time.

### 2.25.9 Bought, not yet given

Between the idea and the day it is handed over the present sits in a cupboard. Noted only as an
idea, it would stay on the shopping list and somebody else might buy it again. Decided with the
maintainer on 2026-11-17 (concept *Geschenkideen für später*, E3).

- **Bought** is a state of its own between *idea* and *given*, reached only from an idea —
  the same record, title, note, link and time kept. *Bought* sits on each idea, on the card and
  on `/gifts`; a bought gift offers *Mark as given…* and *Not bought yet*, which makes it an idea
  again, as it was.
- **Off the list and the counts.** A bought gift leaves `/gifts`, the *Ideas* tab's count, the
  *n ideas* line on Home, the brief and the calendar link: it is no longer something to get.
- **Still in reach.** On the person's card it waits under *Ready to give · n*, below the open
  ideas, each row marked with the bag icon. In Home's *Coming up* (§2.13.3) the person's row
  says *Ready: Scarf, Teapot* until it is given — so the present is not forgotten in the
  cupboard. It is read with the same visibility as every gift (`readyGifts`).
- **Undo.** *Bought* and *Not bought yet* take the row away at once and the toast offers *Undo*
  for the same few seconds a removal has (§2.23); only then is `gift.markBought` sent. Like
  rewriting, it waits for Stella (docs/dev/04 §4.11.2). A given or received gift cannot be
  marked bought.
- **Besorgt… or Erledigt (D17).** Who does it decides the word. A gift the household hands over
  itself — nobody named, *Wir*, or one of the household's own people — is *Bought…* (*Besorgt…*):
  a small sheet asks *Where is it kept? (optional)*, the places already used offered as chips,
  latest first, up to eight, the field free for a new one. Anyone else's gift — an aunt, a giver
  the reader may not see, one who has left — is *Done* (*Erledigt*) in one tap: the household
  only notes that it is taken care of, so there is nothing to ask. Its way back reads *Not done
  yet* (*Doch noch nicht erledigt*); the state is the same *bought* either way.
- **Where it waits (D20).** The place reads on the bought row beside the box icon. It belongs to
  the waiting: *Not bought yet* clears it, and a given gift no longer shows it. The places offered
  are those of the gifts the reader may see (`giftPlaces`).
- **Kept from them (D23).** On a member's own person page the sheet offers the gift's author *Keep
  it from Mama*, ticked: a present in the cupboard is a surprise even when the idea was not.
  Leaving it ticked hides the gift as §2.25.10 does; unticking lets them see it. Only the author
  is asked, as ever; anyone else's *Bought…* leaves the choice as it was.

### 2.25.10 Everyone but the person it is for

A family where everyone is a member gives presents to members: the idea for Mama's birthday
must not stand on Mama's own page. Decided with the maintainer on 2026-11-17 (E9).

- **A third choice of visibility**, beside *Shared* and *Private*: *Everyone but Mama*. It is
  offered only when the person is a member's own record (their *This is me*, §2.10) and that
  member is not the one writing; a private gift cannot be one, and only the author may choose or
  clear it.
- **Kept from them everywhere.** The member it is for never sees the gift: not on their own
  card, not its *soon* or *due* chip or its *Still current?*, not in any count, on `/gifts`, on
  Home, in the brief, in their calendar link, in search, nor — once given — in their story.
  Everyone else who may see the person sees it as a shared gift, marked *hidden from Mama* with
  the eye icon. The rule is the central one (`canViewGift`, `giftVisibleTo`, docs/dev/03
  §3.7), so every read keeps it.
- **For good.** Given, it stays kept from them; the author can make it shared afterwards.
- **The archive** (§2.15) carries it marked (`hidden_from_recipient`), as it carries a private
  record: the admin's backup is the household taking its data out, not a member reading it.
- **One's own gift.** A member who notes a gift on their own record is refused the choice —
  they would hide it from themselves.
- **Offline.** `gift.add` carries the choice as `hiddenFromRecipient`; a command kept by an
  older device without it is a shared gift, as before.

### 2.25.11 One idea for several people

The same puzzle for the fifth birthday of Mia, Ben and Lea is one idea, not three (owner
decision 2026-11-18, ADR-134). It is noted on its own page, `/gifts/several`, and followed up from any of its people in *Who has
it?*.

- **A gift each.** Noting it gives every person an ordinary gift of their own — on their card,
  on `/gifts`, in the calendar — bought, given or dropped on its own. A group binds them and
  keeps what they share: the title, the note, the link, the time as noted and the occasion.
- **The time per person.** *For the 5th birthday* or *Christmas at 6* is worked out for each
  (§2.25.8); one may have a time of their own instead. Whoever it cannot fit — already past it,
  or without a birth year for an age — is left out and named; the others are still noted. If it
  fits nobody, nothing is noted. At most 50 people, each once.
- **Visibility.** All of a group's gifts share its visibility. A surprise for several (§2.25.10)
  is kept from each member it is for — they see none of the group's gifts, not only their own;
  the author cannot make one that includes their own person.
- **A merge** keeps one open idea per person in a group: the merged person's duplicate leaves
  the group and stays as an idea of its own.
- **The archive** (§2.15) carries the group once, each gift naming it.

**Noting it** (`/gifts/several`, screen in docs/dev/05 §5.5.19):

- **Ways in.** The Gifts card's **⋯** menu, *For several…*, opens the page for that person,
  ticked; the palette's *Gift idea for several …* asks with whom to begin (docs/dev/05 §5.4).
  The ordinary *+ Idea* form stays one person (D9).
- **Who.** A name search adds one person at a time. Shortcuts from the person it was opened
  from tick a set at once and untick it again: *Children of Anna* for each parent (their own
  children when they are the parent), *Siblings of Mia*, each home they live in, each circle
  they are in. A set of one, or past 50 people, is not offered; shortcuts are not stored.
- **When** is the ordinary picker (§2.25.8), every age offered, since each person's line says
  the rest.
- **A line per person** says what the time comes to for them, soonest first, *now* before any
  day, those it does not fit last and grey (D3), each with a tick at the right end. A grey row
  says how to help it: without a birth year, *Age today, about* — an age typed stands in for an
  estimated birth year, written on the person when saved (D5) — or *Enter birthday*; already
  past it, *instead:* the next birthday (Christmas this year for *Christmas at N*, D1) or *now*,
  which gives that person a time of their own (*own time*, *reset*).
- **The footer** counts *For 3* or *For 3 of 4* and names who is left out and why (D2), with the
  visibility — *Surprise* only when a chosen person is a member's own — and *Save*.
- **Saved** through the outbox (`giftGroup.add`, docs/dev/04 §4.11.2): the toast says *Noted for
  3*, or *Noted for 2 · not Emma* for anyone the server could not fit, and the page lands on the
  Gifts card of the person it was opened from, else the first.

**Following it up** (*Who has it?*, screen in docs/dev/05 §5.5.20):

- **On the card.** A gift of a group reads *also for Ben and Lea ›* under its line, naming the
  others it is for; a tap opens *Who has it?* — a sheet on a phone, a dialog on a desktop (D7).
- **One row per person**, given first by their day, then the open ones soonest first: *given 20 January
  (Birthday)*, or what the time comes to for them, *bought ·* before it, with *own time* or *own
  title* where theirs differs from the group's. *Mark as given…* gives that one gift; the row's
  **⋯** has *Not bought*, the person's card, *Detach from the others* (the gift stays as an idea
  of its own) and, while open, *Remove for Mia*; *Not bought* and *Remove* wait out their
  *Undo* window.
- **+ Person** adds people at the group's time, worked out and previewed for each as when noting
  it (F11); title, note and link come along, and whoever it does not fit stays grey in the preview
  (`giftGroup.addRecipients`).
- **Bought for everyone (3)** marks every open idea bought, with *Undo* (`giftGroup.markBought`).
- **✎ Change** edits the title, note, link and time for every open gift (*Changed for 3 open*),
  or only for the person whose card it opened on. A new time is previewed per person; one it no
  longer fits keeps its own (D6), a bought gift keeps the time it was bought for, and a given gift
  stays as it was given (`giftGroup.edit`).
- **The ⋯ menu** dissolves the group — every gift goes on alone (`giftGroup.dissolve`) — or
  removes the open gifts, with *Undo*, keeping the given ones (`giftGroup.removeOpen`). Once
  everyone has it, the panel says so; only the story is left.
- These go straight to Stella, as `gift.edit` does: somebody may already have seen the gifts.
- **On `/gifts`** each gift stays under its own person, with the same *also for …* line; it
  leads to their Gifts card with *Who has it?* open (`?group=`).
- **In the calendar link** (§2.13.4) the gifts of a group with the same title on the same day
  are one event naming everyone, *Gift idea: Lego train (Ben, Mia)* (F6), its id from the group
  and the day, linked to *Who has it?*; a gift whose title was changed for them alone keeps an
  event of its own. A surprise stays out of each member's feed it is for, as everywhere.

### 2.25.12 Who gives it

Grandma gives Tomy the puzzle; the household gives him the bike. An open gift is *vergeben* to
whoever gives it, so two people never buy one present (owner decisions D3, D13, D15, D19,
ADR-140).

- **A person, or *Wir*.** The giver is always a person record — a grandparent, an aunt, a child
  of the household — or the household itself (*Wir*). Nobody named is *us* too, as every gift was
  before; *Wir* says it on purpose. Never the person it is for, never someone the writer cannot see.
- **Vergeben…** on an open idea or a bought one asks *Wer schenkt?* and *Zu welchem Anlass?* (the
  ordinary when, §2.25.8). Chosen again, it moves the gift on, saying whom it replaces; *Wieder
  frei* clears it. One row per present, so a booking is just this field: choosing other givers
  replaces the earlier ones, never joins them. A given gift keeps its givers; it happened.
- **Several together.** Several people can give one present: Julia and Andy to Hilde. *Von* /
  *Wer schenkt?* is a person search and nothing else; each person found stays as a chip with a ×,
  and the search stays open for the next. *Wir* is an entry of its own and mixes with people. The
  first named is the giver (`giver_contact_id`, or `giver_is_household` when *Wir* is among them,
  which then comes first); the others sit in `gift_co_giver`, in the order named, each once. Each
  giver's *Gives* tab lists the gift, adding *gemeinsam mit Andy*. A household member counts as
  handing it over once any of the givers is ours (D17).
- **Optional, and quiet.** Who gave a gift is rarely worth saying, so *+ Given* shows *Von* as one
  line — *From us · Default* — with *Change*, and opens the search only then. Nothing is stored
  while it stays closed: nobody named is *us*. With the viewer's own person as the only giver the
  line reads *From me*; several, or anyone else, read *From* and the names. *Back to default*
  empties the givers. *Vergeben…* needs a giver, so its field stays open.
- **Whom it offers.** *Me* (German *Mir*; the viewer's own person) first, then *Us* (German *Uns*), then the person's own links, both ways — their children among
  them — then the household: each member's own person and their children. They are the list's
  first entries, shown once the search is focused, never pushed as chips beside the field; the
  living only, each once, never the person it is for. The search finds anyone else, and names
  someone new right there (*+ New person*, §2.2).
- **On the forms.** *+ Idea* asks *Wer schenkt?* under *Note or link*; *+ Given* asks *Von*. A
  received gift has no giver: it came from its person.
- **A surprise.** Somebody giving it, a gift for a member is a surprise (§2.25.10): picking a giver
  on a shared one turns *Everyone but Mama* on, and *Vergeben…* offers its author *Keep it from
  Mama*, ticked the first time. Only the author decides, as ever.
- **On the row.** *Sandra schenkt* / *Wir schenken* on an open gift, *von Sandra* / *von uns* on
  a given one; with several, *Julia und Andy schenken* / *von Julia und Andy*. A giver the reader may not see reads *vergeben* or *von jemand anderem* — nameless,
  and their id never reaches the page.
- **Deleted, merged.** Deleting the giver keeps their name on the gift (*Ruth schenkt*); a merge
  repoints it to the kept person, and drops it where the kept person would give to themselves, to
  a giver the gift already has, or to the person it is for.
- **The archive** (§2.15) carries the giver, the *Wir* flag, a left name and the other givers
  (`co_givers`); an older archive restores every gift with none, and a giver it did not bring
  along as nobody.
- **Offline.** `gift.add` carries the giver and the others beside them (`coGivers`); *Vergeben…* (`gift.assign`) waits for Stella, since
  someone else may have taken the gift meanwhile.

### 2.25.13 The card by occasion

Christmas at the grandparents' is one plan: who gives what, what is done, what is still free. Once
any open or bought gift on the card has a giver or a day, the *Ideas* tab reads by occasion
(owner study 2026-11, D1–D2, `byOccasion` in `src/lib/gifts/occasions.ts`); until then it stays
as §2.25.2 describes.

- **The occasion** is the day a gift's when falls on — a set birthday, a Christmas at an age, a
  day (§2.25.8). Gifts for the same day share a group, soonest first; *Without an occasion* comes
  last and holds the rest, *now* and *from age* among them. An age without a birth year has no
  day, so its gift has no occasion.
- **The heading** names it as the when chip does, *Christmas 2026* for a Christmas Eve, and how
  near it is: *in 33 days · 24 Dec*, *tomorrow*, *today*, *passed*. At its right the tally,
  *1 done · 2 assigned · 1 free*: bought, given to someone, nobody yet. *Without an occasion* has
  no tally, since nothing there is due.
- **In a group** the open gifts come first and the bought ones last, slightly faded, so what is
  left to do leads. An idea nobody gives says *still free* (*noch frei*) under its title. Ideas
  still waiting for their time stay under the *Later* fold, and *Ready to give* is no heading of
  its own any more — a bought gift stands with its occasion.


### 2.25.14 Recording what was given

After Christmas nobody notes eight presents one by one. *Nachtragen…* records what was given at
an occasion that has passed, several gifts at once, in one sheet (owner study 2026-11, D9–D12;
`recordedCommands` in `src/lib/gifts/record-given.ts`).

- **Where it opens.** From the Gifts card's ⋯ menu, *Mehrere nachtragen…*. From a passed
  occasion's group, where *Weihnachten 2025 ist vorbei. Nachtragen…* stands under its heading.
  And from the header of `/gifts`, for everyone at once. It does not open from Home.
- **The occasion.** Chips offer the last Christmas Eve, the person's last birthday where it is
  known, and every passed day a planned gift is for, latest first, four at most. *Anderer Tag…*
  asks for a day, up to today, and that day has no occasion. Every gift in the
  sheet is given on that day, with that occasion.
- **What was planned.** The open and bought gifts for that day are listed, each with its
  checkbox. A gift that is bought or has a giver starts ticked. A free idea starts unticked,
  since it may never have been given. A ticked gift is marked given, the same as *Verschenkt*
  (`gift.markGiven`).
- **What else was given.** Free rows read *Was · Für · Von*. On the card, *Für* is the person.
  On `/gifts`, *Für* is a person search or *Uns (Haushalt)*. *Von* starts at *Wir*. On the card
  it offers the person's links, as §2.25.12 describes. On `/gifts` it offers only *Wir* and the
  search, because without a recipient there is nobody to take links from. *+ Zeile* adds a row.
  A row with no title is left out. The *already given* hint (§2.25.2) appears on the card only.
- **For us.** A row *Für: Uns* is a gift the household received. It is noted on its giver's
  contact as received, so it needs a person under *Von*, and the sheet says so until it has one.
- **Shared.** Every gift noted here is shared. Once a gift has been given, there is no surprise
  left to keep (D12).
- **One command per gift.** The sheet posts once, and the server applies a command for each gift
  in order: `gift.markGiven` for each ticked gift, then a `gift.add` for each row. Each command is
  judged on its own, exactly as it would be if sent alone. The whole sheet is checked before
  anything is sent. If a command is refused partway through, the ones already applied stay
  applied. The sheet then stays open and says how many went in. It does not use the outbox: like
  *Vergeben…*, the batch needs Stella to be reachable.
- **Afterwards.** A toast says *3 Geschenke zu Weihnachten 2025 eingetragen*. The batch has no
  *Undo*; each gift can still be edited or deleted on its card.

### 2.25.15 What a person gives

Every person's Gifts card has a fourth tab, *Schenkt* (*Gives*), after *Received*, children
included (owner study 2026-11, D14). It lists the gifts whose giver is this person
(`giver_contact_id`, §2.25.12), across every recipient: open ones first, then *Früher* for the
given ones. Each row reads title, *für Sandra* (a link to the recipient), the when of an open
idea, and the day and occasion of a given one. The count shows once there is a gift. The tab
appears whenever the strip does, and a person who gives something but has no gifts of their own
shows the strip too.

- **Adding.** *+ Tomy schenkt…*, in the card's ⋯ menu, opens the idea form with a required
  *Für wen?* (`GiftGiverField` with `name="for"` and no *Wir*; a person can be created in the
  search) and a *When* limited to a day or *later*, since the recipient's age is not known there. It
  posts to `?/addGiftBy`: a `gift.add` for the recipient, an idea, with the giver forced to the
  page's person. It has no outbox, so it needs Stella to be reachable. A gift for a member of
  the household starts as a surprise, as every giver's gift does (D4).
- **Surprise.** A gift kept from its recipient (§2.25.10) is hidden from that member in this
  tab too. `listGivenByVisibleTo` joins through `giftVisibleTo`, so the member whose own person is
  the recipient never gets the row, nor its count. The others see it with *Kept from Mama*.
  The access test lives in `gift-repository.test.ts`.
- **A giver who left.** A deleted giver's gifts keep the read-only `giver_left_name`
  (§2.25.12); they have no tab to appear on.
