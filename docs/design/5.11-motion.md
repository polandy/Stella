## 5.11 Motion

Something that opens or closes **in place** — a folded row, a form under its card's header, a
line that turns into its editor, a list that unfolds — moves one way everywhere, so the page
reads as the same paper changing shape rather than as a cut. The phone map's grow and shrink in
the People card (§5.5) is where it started; everything else now moves like it.

### 5.11.1 The rules

- **One glide, one fade, one easing.** A height changes in **300 ms** (`--motion-expand`), what
  appears or goes fades in **200 ms** (`--motion-fade`), both on **`cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1)`**
  (`--ease-standard`, Tailwind's `ease-standard`) — quick to start, gentle to land, opening and
  closing alike. The values live in `app.css` and, for motion computed in script, as `MOTION` in
  `src/lib/design/tokens.ts`; a test holds the two halves together. No component writes a
  duration of its own.
- **Content is ready before it shows.** What opens is rendered at its final size first and
  fades in while the box grows over it — no blank frame, no flash, and nothing that sizes or
  fits itself once the motion has ended. Two alternatives in one place (a line and its editor)
  **cross-fade over each other** while the box glides from one height to the other.
- **The surroundings hold still.** Only what is below the change moves, and it glides: the card's
  top and the button that was pressed stay put. The page scrolls only when that button would
  otherwise leave the screen — *Show fewer* under a list that just folded — and then smoothly,
  after the motion. A form that has grown is brought fully into view the same way.
- **Opening a form brings it into view one way, on every card.** *Log contact*, *Add note*,
  *Add photos*, the People card's `+` and *How are we connected?*, an identity-card row's *Add*
  and *Write a moment* all open through `showOpenedForm` (`motion.svelte.ts`), with the decision
  in `glideToOpenedForm` (`motion.ts`): a card whose top is in the **upper half** of the visible
  band (under the top bar and the jump bar, above a phone's keyboard) **holds still** — there is
  room for the form below its header, and a jump would only unsettle. A card whose top is lower,
  slipped under the bar, or off screen **glides its top to just under the bar**, smoothly, so
  the form opens where it is read and, on a phone, above the keyboard. The cursor then goes into
  the first field without a scroll of its own (`preventScroll`). Once the form has grown
  (`settleOpenedForm`), a card that held still has all of its form brought into view
  (`block: 'nearest'`, also smooth); a card that glided is glided to the top once more, because
  near the foot of the page the first glide stopped where the page ended before the form made it
  longer. The person page's jump bar goes to a card by the same rule (`bringCardIntoView`, the
  same glide without a field to focus), instead of an anchor's jump.
- **Reversible.** Pressing again mid-way turns the motion round from where it is, in either
  direction; nothing restarts from an end.
- **Reduced motion is respected.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion` everything switches at once —
  no height animation, no fade, no smooth scroll.
- **Focus is not held up.** Each place keeps its own focus rule (a form takes the cursor at once,
  a closed one hands it back to its button); the motion runs underneath. What is on its way out
  is inert from the first frame and gives up the cursor at once, as it did when it simply went.
- **No sideways scroll and no stray layout shift.** The animating box clips its content while it
  moves (`overflow: clip`, so a focused field cannot scroll it); it is never given `contain`,
  because several of these boxes hold full-screen overlays (`position: fixed`) that would be
  trapped by it. A block appearing in a flex column or grid takes the parent's gap up with a
  negative margin while it is closed, so the gap glides in with it instead of arriving whole.

### 5.11.2 The primitive

`src/lib/motion/` — the decisions in `motion.ts` (pure, unit-tested: the easing curve, each
frame of a reveal, when a height glides at all), the DOM work in `motion.svelte.ts`:

- **`transition:reveal`** — one block appears or goes in place: its height (and padding, border,
  margin) grows from nothing while it fades in. Used on the block itself, so lists and grids
  keep their structure.
- **`use:glide={{ key }}`** — a box whose content changes under it glides from the height on
  screen to the new one whenever `key` changes; anything else that resizes it (a window, a photo
  loading) does not. It publishes `data-motion="moving" | "settled"` and an `onsettled` callback.
- **`Swap`** (`src/lib/components/Swap.svelte`) — `glide` plus a cross-fade for two alternatives
  in one place: `<Swap when={editing}>form{#snippet otherwise()}line{/snippet}</Swap>`.

The phone map keeps its own CSS transition on the same tokens: its frame glides between two
fixed heights and the live map must be mounted only once the frame has arrived (§5.5), which a
generic box cannot know.

### 5.11.3 Where it applies

- `Section` (§5.7): a row's fold and unfold (Contact, Tags, How we met, and the rest), and
  every card's add form — log contact, a note, a relationship, a photo, a circle member.
- The identity card: the quiet button (*Add address, phone, email …*) unfolding the rows it
  brings; the dates', the address's and the circles' editors unfolding in place of their facts;
  the name and the job turning into their editors and back (`Swap`); the ⋯ menu's confirm
  steps — archive, merge, delete.
- The People card: *Show N more* / *Show fewer* and edit mode (the list glides between its two
  heights and *Show more* rides on its lower edge), a row's edit form under its tile, the *How
  are we connected?* picker, and the add form's one-date / one-per-person choice.
- Elsewhere: the journal's new-entry form and an entry turning into its editor, the new-circle
  form, a relationship type's edit forms and its "from the other side" field, the new-person
  form's relative hint, Home's link suggestion going and *Show all* under *Coming up*, and the
  unsent-changes question when signing out.

### 5.11.4 Where it does not

- **Between screens** — the view transition's cross-fade (§5.5) is its own.
- **Toasts, menus and popovers, dialogs** — they float above the page and move nothing in it;
  they keep their own short entrances.
- **Swaps inside a line** — a value turning into its field (`InlineEdit`), *Discard* turning
  into its question on a kept change, the checkboxes of a select mode: the line keeps its
  height, so there is nothing to glide, and a fade would only delay reading the control.
- **The selection bars** of select mode are fixed to the screen's foot, like a sheet.
- **Native `<details>`** (*more details* on a new person, declined suggestions, *who is who*)
  open the browser's way; animating them works in one engine only.
- **An answered row leaving a list** has its own, measured motion that keeps the reader's
  place (`src/lib/ui/leaving.ts`, docs/02 §2.4.1).
- **The graph's overlays** (path prompt, node and group peeks) float over the canvas.
