## 5.3 Typography, spacing, radius, elevation

- **Two typefaces, two jobs.**
  - **Instrument Sans** (`--font-sans`) carries the interface: navigation, labels, buttons,
    lists, forms.
  - **Newsreader** (`--font-serif`) carries what a person wrote: moments, journal entries,
    notes, and the composer's own field. A written line should never read like a data field —
    that difference is what separates a moment from a system event in the stream.
  - Both are **self-hosted**: the `@fontsource-variable/*` packages ship the woff2 files and
    Vite rewrites the `@font-face` URLs to our own origin (no CDN, per §4.7). They are imported
    once in the root layout. The system stack stays as the fallback.
- **Scale (rem):** 0.75 / 0.8125 / 0.875 / 1 / 1.125 / 1.375 / 1.75 / 2.25. Headings tighten to
  −0.02em; serif body copy sits at 17px/1.5. Comfortable line length (~65ch) for note bodies;
  a moment's body in the stream is capped at 72ch, so a full-width stream (§5.5) never runs a
  line past a thousand pixels.
- **The uppercase label is a divider inside a list, nothing else.** Small (11–12px), uppercase,
  letter-spaced and quiet, it splits one list into runs: the stream's and the journal's day
  dividers, the People card's group headings (*Family · 7*, *Also related*) and
  the pills that stand for them in its fold, a circle's role groups, the People list's letters,
  the groups of a picker list. It never stands alone. A **section heading** — a rail band
  (*Coming up*), a sub-band of a card (*On group photos*), a panel (*Also true?*, *Suggestions*,
  *Already in Stella?*), a section of a dialog — is sentence case `text-sm font-semibold` in `--fg`, the
  style the cards' own headings use. A **label that belongs to a control** — the stream filter's
  *What* and *Who* — is quiet sentence case `text-sm font-medium` in `--fg-muted`, part of its
  row. So the style alone tells a band from a divider inside one. Status badges (*New person*)
  are badges, not labels, and are not bound by this.
- **Spacing:** 4px base grid (Tailwind default). Sections 32px, card padding 16–18px,
  list rows 8–10px.
- **Radius:** `--radius` 12px for cards, `--radius-control` 8px for buttons and inputs, full
  round for avatars and pills.
- **Elevation:** cards sit on `--card` above `--bg` and are lifted by `--shadow-card`, a
  text-tinted shadow; `--shadow-pop` is for menus and popovers. Borders are for inputs and
  dividers, not for separating cards.

## 5.4 Layout & navigation

- **App shell:**
  - **Desktop:** left sidebar (Home, People, Circles, Graph, Settings), a top bar with the
    search button that opens the ⌘K palette and the *Add person* button, content area.
    *Add person* is a **secondary** (outlined) button — icon and label from `md`, the icon
    alone on a phone, its accessible name kept — because the one filled primary on a screen
    is the page's own action: the pencil on Home, *Write a moment* on a person (§5.1).
    On a phone's **Home** the search button is left out: the *Find a person* field right
    under the bar is the search there, and two doors to the same list would only have to be
    learned as the same. Every other phone page keeps it; ⌘K works everywhere.
  - **Mobile:** bottom tab bar (Home, People, *pencil*, Circles, Graph). The centre action is
    the **pencil** — writing a moment is the verb the app is built around, adding a record
    is not — and it opens the composer as a sheet over Home (§2.22.1). Settings, rarely
    opened, sits in the top bar there rather than taking a sixth place.
    The shell is as tall as the **visible** viewport (`100dvh`), not `100vh`: a phone browser
    counts `100vh` with its address bar hidden, and a shell that tall runs its foot — the
    graph's peek strip, say — under the fixed tab bar while the address bar shows.
  - **Breadcrumb trail** in the top bar, derived from the route + loaded data
    (`Home / People / {name} / Journal`). Every segment links, so Home is always one
    click away; on Home itself there is no trail — "Home" alone would repeat the tab bar — and
    a phone, having no sidebar, shows the logo there instead. **Below `md` only the parent
    crumb shows**, as a back link (*‹ People*): the current page's name is its card's headline
    a little lower, and the whole trail would wrap a long name onto a second line and make the
    bar taller than on other pages. From `md` the full trail.
  - **On a phone the top bar gets out of the way**: it slides up while the page scrolls down
    and comes back as soon as it scrolls up (`TOP_BAR_TRAVEL_PX` in one direction, so a
    shaky thumb does not flicker it), and always shows within its own height of the top.
    Within its height of the bottom it stays as it is: hiding it there makes the page taller
    and pulls the scroll back, which would bring it back and hide it again in a loop.
    The rule is the pure `followScroll` (`src/lib/shell/top-bar.ts`); reduced motion gets a
    cut instead of the slide.
    The active destination is marked with `aria-current="page"` in the sidebar and tab bar. The desktop shell's account menu (theme + sign out) lives in the
    shell, not per page; sign out is repeated as a plain button on **Settings** so it is
    reachable on mobile, where that menu is not rendered.
  - The shell is a single `(app)/+layout.svelte`; pages render content only — no per-page
    headers or back links.
- **Responsive, mobile-first.** Capture flows are optimized for one-handed phone use.
- **Command palette** (⌘K / Ctrl+K, or the search button): a native `<dialog>` listing
  actions (*Write a moment*, *Gift idea for …*, *Add person*) and the people the viewer may
  see, narrowed as you type with arrow keys and Enter; a typed query always ends in *Search
  everything*, which is the full-text search over notes the palette itself does not read.
  *Gift idea for …* is a second step in the same dialog rather than a page: the search icon
  becomes a back arrow, the field empties and asks *Gift idea for whom?*, and only people
  are listed, each landing on their Gifts card's open idea form (docs/02 §2.25.5); Backspace
  in the empty field goes back. The rows come from a
  pure `paletteRows`; the people arrive with the app shell's `load`, so the first keystroke
  answers without a round trip. A person who shares their name with someone else gets a
  second line saying which one they are (docs/02 §2.2.3). It is the one control here that cannot work without
  JavaScript, so its trigger stays **disabled until the shell has mounted** rather than
  swallowing a click in the first moments after a load — which is also what lets a test know
  the page is ready instead of waiting and hoping (`docs/08` §8.4).
